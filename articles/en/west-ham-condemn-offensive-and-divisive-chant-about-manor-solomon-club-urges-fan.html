West Ham condemn 'offensive and divisive' chant about Manor Solomon — club urges fans to stop

8/20/2026 · By Koora Live

West Ham United has publicly condemned an "offensive and divisive" chant aimed at winger Manor Solomon that was heard during their match at Burnley last weekend. According to BBC Sport, the club has urged supporters to cease the "anti-Palestine" chant and to respect players and fellow fans.

What was reported at Burnley

BBC Sport's report states that during the visit to Burnley last weekend a chant targeting Manor Solomon—described as "anti-Palestine"—could be heard, prompting a response from West Ham. The club issued a public message calling the chant offensive and divisive and asking supporters to stop. Beyond this reporting, the precise timing or frequency of the chant has not been detailed here.

Why clubs act strongly against discriminatory chants

Professional football clubs routinely take a public stance when incidents of discriminatory or politically charged chanting arise. Teams have responsibilities both to protect their players from abuse and to preserve a safe, inclusive environment for all supporters, staff and visitors. Manor Solomon is a West Ham player, and the club’s intervention reflects a broader expectation in the game that discriminatory behaviour—whether aimed at ethnicity, nationality, religion or political stance—should not be tolerated in stadiums or around matches.

Beyond moral considerations, clubs are mindful of their role in the community and the message they send to younger fans. Public condemnation and clear appeals to supporters serve to set behavioural standards and to reassure stakeholders that such conduct is not acceptable in line with modern football’s commitment to inclusion.

Analysis

West Ham’s decision to call out the chant publicly, as reported by BBC Sport, can be seen as an attempt to draw a line under behaviour the club deems harmful to team unity and its public image. Analysts might argue that swift, visible responses are part of a contemporary club’s duty to its squad and fanbase — they serve both to protect individuals and to reinforce the norms expected within a stadium environment. At the same time, public appeals depend on buy-in from supporters; long-term change typically requires coordinated efforts across clubs, supporter groups and governing bodies to address the root causes of discriminatory chants.

The incident at Burnley prompted a clear response from West Ham, underlining the club’s rejection of chants it regards as offensive and divisive. As BBC Sport’s coverage shows, such episodes continue to challenge clubs, fans and the football authorities to uphold standards of conduct and to protect players from targeted abuse.

What can clubs do when discriminatory chants occur?

In general, clubs may publicly condemn discriminatory behaviour, make stadium announcements asking supporters to stop, work with stewards to identify and remove offenders, offer support to targeted players, and cooperate with authorities or governing bodies if further action is required. These are common measures used across football to address abusive chanting, though the BBC Sport item reports only that West Ham made a public call for the chant to stop in this instance.
