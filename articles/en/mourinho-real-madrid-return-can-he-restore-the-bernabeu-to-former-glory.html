Mourinho Real Madrid return: Can he restore the Bernabeu to former glory?

8/12/2026 · By Koora Live

Jose Mourinho has returned to Real Madrid 13 years after his first departure, and the headline question is a familiar one: can the 63-year-old deliver the trophies that supporters expect at the Santiago Bernabeu? BBC Sport has revisited this story and asked whether Mourinho can reproduce the impact of his earlier stint and lead the club back to sustained success.

This piece outlines what Mourinho’s arrival means in broad terms, the strengths he brings as a manager, the obstacles he is likely to face in Madrid, and how fans and observers might judge his progress over the coming months.

What Mourinho brings to Real Madrid

Mourinho is one of the most recognisable figures in modern football management, known for a pragmatic approach to tactics, intense attention to detail and a strong record of success across multiple leagues. While the BBC Sport report highlights his return 13 years after leaving, more generally Mourinho is associated with a managerial profile that prioritises organisation, defensive solidity and a clear plan for matches.

At a club like Real Madrid — one of world football’s most storied institutions with a long history of domestic and continental achievement — a manager with Mourinho’s experience can be appealing because of his familiarity with the pressures and expectations of top-level European competition. His presence alone often reshapes the public narrative around a club, bringing renewed media focus and a renewed sense of urgency inside the dressing room.

Challenges awaiting at the Bernabeu

High-profile returns are rarely straightforward. Real Madrid operate in an environment of relentless expectation: supporters demand silverware, the media scrutinise every decision, and the margin for error is slim. Balancing the immediate need to produce results with longer-term squad planning can be especially tricky for any incoming coach, no matter their pedigree.

Beyond the Broader pressures, modern football clubs also present tactical and personnel puzzles that differ from previous eras. Opponents study elite managers intensely, transfer markets evolve quickly and clubs must juggle domestic and European calendars. For Mourinho, the task will be to adapt his well-known methods to the current realities of the squad and competition, while managing personalities, form and the club’s ambition for trophies.

Analysis

Opinion: Mourinho’s return to Real Madrid, as reported by BBC Sport, is symbolically significant. It reconnects a club that has frequently looked to established names to navigate transitional periods with a coach who has previously operated in that exact environment. Analysts might see this appointment as a statement of intent from a club seeking familiarity and a clear managerial voice. However, it is also reasonable to frame expectations conservatively: past success is no guarantee of future trophies, and the football landscape — tactically, commercially and culturally — has continued to evolve since Mourinho’s first spell.

Mourinho’s homecoming to Real Madrid, as covered by BBC Sport, revives familiar debates about experience versus evolution, short-term impact versus long-term planning, and the price of success at one of football’s biggest clubs. Supporters and commentators will now watch closely to see how this new chapter unfolds and whether the club returns to the levels of performance its history demands.
