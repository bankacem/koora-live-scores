BBC Sport has taken a closer look at how the three relegated sides — West Ham, Burnley and Wolves — will navigate the Championship this season. The drop into the second tier presents each club with familiar challenges: adapting to a longer, more attritional schedule, managing squad turnover and targeting a return to the top tier as efficiently as possible.

Club profiles: West Ham, Burnley and Wolves at a glance

West Ham United, Burnley and Wolverhampton Wanderers are three well-known names in English football and, as BBC Sport notes, they are the relegated trio facing life in the Championship this season. Each club brings a distinct identity and fanbase into the second tier. West Ham play their home matches at the London Stadium (the club's home since the mid-2010s) and are recognised for a sizeable supporter base drawn from east London and beyond. Burnley, based at Turf Moor (their historic ground since the late 19th century), are traditionally associated with a compact, community-focused club culture. Wolves play at Molineux, a stadium with deep roots in the Midlands.

All three clubs carry histories that make them notable presences in the English game: Burnley were founded in the 19th century and have long-standing local traditions, Wolverhampton Wanderers trace their roots back to the late 19th century as well, and West Ham have origins from the turn of the 20th century. Those institutional backgrounds often shape how clubs react to relegation — from how they manage finances to how they plan for a promotion push. BBC Sport’s piece frames the upcoming Championship campaign as one where these characteristics will be tested against the unique demands of the division.

The Championship challenge and what it means for relegated clubs

The Championship differs from higher divisions in style and scheduling: it is a 24-team league with a 46-game regular season, known for its intensity, fixture congestion and physical demands. Promotion is awarded to the top two finishers, while teams finishing third to sixth contest playoffs for the final promotion place — a structure that rewards consistency across a long season as well as peak form in the run-in. For teams arriving from a higher division, early adaptation to these rhythms is often decisive.

Relegated clubs typically face three immediate tasks. First, stabilising the squad by deciding which players to keep, which to sell and where reinforcements are needed. Second, setting realistic sporting targets — balancing the ambition to return to the top tier quickly with the financial and sporting realities of the Championship. Third, maintaining a strong relationship with supporters, who can be a critical factor in home form. BBC Sport’s assessment encourages a focus on how each club’s resources, squad continuity and strategic choices will shape their season in the second tier.

Analysis

Analysts might see the presence of three established clubs in the Championship as raising the competitive baseline: each club brings infrastructure, fan support and professional experience that can make them immediate contenders. That said, relegation often triggers financial and personnel shifts that can blunt short-term prospects. The key variables will be squad continuity, recruitment targets that fit the Championship profile, and an early run of results to build momentum.

From a strategic point of view, clubs that balance ambition with pragmatism — protecting core assets while investing selectively where the squad lacks depth — often perform best. BBC Sport’s overview implies that this season will be a test of how quickly West Ham, Burnley and Wolves can reconfigure themselves for the peculiar grind of the second tier, and whether they can translate institutional strengths into on-pitch consistency over 46 matches.

BBC Sport’s look at West Ham, Burnley and Wolves highlights that relegation marks the start of a different kind of campaign — one where organisational strength, squad management and adaptation to the Championship’s demands will determine which clubs position themselves best for a return to higher levels of the game.
