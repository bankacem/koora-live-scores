Scottish FA Withdraws Support for Gianni Infantino — Impact on FIFA Re-election Prospects

8/17/2026 · By Koora Live

The Scottish Football Association has publicly announced it no longer has confidence in FIFA president Gianni Infantino and will not cast a vote for his re-election, chief executive Ian Maxwell told BBC Sport. This withdrawal of support from a national association is a notable development in the lead-up to any FIFA presidential contest and raises questions about wider sentiment among member federations.

What the Scottish FA said and how it was communicated

According to BBC Sport, the Scottish Football Association's stance was communicated by its chief executive, Ian Maxwell, who stated that the SFA had lost confidence in Gianni Infantino and would not be voting for his re-election. The message was delivered directly to the media outlet and represents an explicit decision by Scotland's governing body for the game.

National associations speak for their memberships when taking positions on governance matters at global football bodies. The SFA's announcement, attributed to its chief executive in reporting by BBC Sport, signals a clear distancing from support of the current FIFA president in advance of any vote on leadership.

Context: the role of national associations and the FIFA presidency

The Scottish Football Association is the governing body for football in Scotland, responsible for managing national teams and representing Scottish interests in international football fora. FIFA is the global governing body for the sport, and its president is elected by representatives of FIFA's member associations.

FIFA presidential elections are decided by votes from those member associations. When a national federation declares it will not back a particular candidate, it is exercising its voting autonomy and making a political statement about governance, leadership and the direction it prefers for world football. Such withdrawals of support from individual associations do not, on their own, determine an election outcome, but they contribute to the narrative and momentum around a contest.

Analysis

This development should be viewed as a strategic statement by the Scottish FA rather than a numerical prediction of any presidential contest. Analysts might see the move as an attempt to influence broader conversation about governance at FIFA and to align Scotland's voice with other federations that have raised concerns in recent years. National associations often weigh domestic priorities, public opinion and wider alliances when deciding whether to support an incumbent.

It is also worth noting that FIFA elections involve the combined voting decisions of many member federations worldwide. While a single association withdrawing support can carry symbolic weight — particularly from a well-established federation like the SFA — the overall electoral picture depends on a much larger set of actors. Observers will likely monitor whether other associations take similar public stances or choose to make their preferences known privately.

The Scottish FA's public withdrawal of support for Gianni Infantino, as reported by BBC Sport and communicated by chief executive Ian Maxwell, is a significant signal from a national governing body. While its practical impact on any FIFA election will depend on the wider international response, the move underlines the ongoing scrutiny of football's global governance and the importance of member associations' voices in shaping leadership decisions.
