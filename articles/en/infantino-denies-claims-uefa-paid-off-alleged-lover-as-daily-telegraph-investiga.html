Infantino denies claims UEFA paid off alleged 'lover' as Daily Telegraph investigation reported

8/8/2026 · By Koora Live

BBC Sport reports that Gianni Infantino has denied claims that UEFA made a severance payment to an individual described as his alleged lover. The allegation, first detailed by the Daily Telegraph, states that the payment was made while Infantino was serving as UEFA general secretary. The dispute has been covered by mainstream outlets and raises questions about governance and transparency within football institutions.

What the Daily Telegraph report says

According to a Daily Telegraph investigation, UEFA provided a severance payment to a person described in coverage as Gianni Infantino's alleged lover at a time when Infantino held the position of general secretary at UEFA. BBC Sport has carried coverage of the story and the headline reports that Infantino denies the claims. Beyond that summary attribution, those are the specific published points available from the reporting cited by BBC Sport and the Daily Telegraph. Readers should note the wording: the term alleged is used in press reports to describe the personal relationship, and the account of a severance payment comes from the Daily Telegraph investigation as referenced by BBC Sport.

Why this matters for football governance

Even when details are limited, allegations involving senior officials and governing bodies inevitably prompt scrutiny because of the responsibilities those organisations carry. UEFA is the governing body for European football, and the role of general secretary is a senior executive position responsible for day-to-day administration. Gianni Infantino later became president of FIFA, the global governing body for the sport, and any questions about past conduct at UEFA tend to attract attention given the influence of these organisations.

Governance standards in football organisations typically emphasise transparency, conflict-of-interest policies and clear records for payments and departures. When media investigations surface claims about severance or other financial arrangements involving senior figures, stakeholders — from national associations to sponsors and fans — often look for clarification from the institutions involved and for independent review where appropriate. That context is why reports like the one from the Daily Telegraph, and BBC Sport's coverage of subsequent denials, are significant beyond the immediate facts reported.
