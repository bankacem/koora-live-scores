Gold family to sell West Ham stake to Amanda Staveley-led consortium - ownership update

8/2/2026 · By Koora Live

BBC Sport reports that the Gold family has agreed to sell a stake in West Ham United to a consortium headed by Amanda Staveley. Vanessa Gold, daughter of former West Ham co-owner David Gold, has confirmed plans for the share sale to the group led by the ex-Newcastle co-owner.

This development marks a significant ownership move for a well-established Premier League club and raises questions about the potential direction and management under new stakeholders. The facts here are those reported by BBC Sport; wider implications can be considered in context but are not presented as confirmed.

The confirmed details: what BBC Sport has reported

According to BBC Sport, Vanessa Gold has confirmed that the Gold family intends to sell shares in West Ham United to a consortium led by Amanda Staveley. The report identifies David Gold as a former co-owner of the club and names Staveley as an ex-Newcastle co-owner who heads the buying group. Those are the explicit, confirmed elements of the story from the BBC's coverage.

No additional specific figures, dates, or contractual terms have been provided in the BBC Sport excerpt referenced here, so any further specifics about the transaction would require direct confirmation from the parties involved or follow-up reporting.

Background on the people and the club involved

West Ham United is a long-established London club that competes in the English top tier and has a passionate supporter base. David Gold was for many years associated with the club in an ownership capacity; Vanessa Gold is his daughter and, according to the BBC report, has confirmed the planned sale of shares.

Amanda Staveley is known in British football circles for her involvement in ownership projects; the BBC excerpt identifies her as a former co-owner of Newcastle United and states she is leading the consortium that plans to acquire the stake. Ownership groups and consortiums are a common structure for investment in Premier League clubs, often bringing together a range of private and institutional backers.
