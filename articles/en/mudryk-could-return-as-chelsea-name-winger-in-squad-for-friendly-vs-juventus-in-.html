Mudryk could return as Chelsea name winger in squad for friendly vs Juventus in Hong Kong

8/4/2026 · By Koora Live

Mykhailo Mudryk could be set for a first outing since a lengthy lay-off after being included in Chelsea's matchday squad for a friendly against Juventus in Hong Kong, according to BBC Sport. The inclusion comes after a 20-month period on the sidelines following a drugs suspension, and his presence on the trip has generated widespread attention.

What the squad selection tells us

BBC Sport reports that Mudryk has been named in Chelsea's squad for the friendly with Juventus in Hong Kong and could return to action on Wednesday. Being listed in a travelling squad for a high-profile pre-season or exhibition match typically indicates that a player is progressing through training and is considered fit enough to take part in team activity. For a player who has not featured for an extended period, a place on the bench or in the squad is often a cautious step before any competitive minutes are considered.

Friendly matches on international tours frequently serve multiple purposes: they provide much-needed match fitness, offer opportunities for fringe or returning players to stake a claim, and allow coaching staff to assess combinations and individual readiness without the full stakes of competitive fixtures. In that context, Mudryk's selection can be interpreted as a sign that Chelsea's staff believe he may be ready to be reintegrated into team environments.

Background and broader context

Mykhailo Mudryk is a high-profile winger who joined Chelsea amid considerable attention; details beyond that are well-known in the broader football community. Chelsea and Juventus are two of Europe's most recognisable clubs, each with long histories in domestic and continental competitions. Both clubs frequently participate in pre-season tours and exhibition matches in Asia, where friendlies are used to engage global fanbases and prepare squads for the coming season.

The report from BBC Sport specifies that Mudryk has been on the sidelines for 20 months following a drugs suspension. Extended absences of that length have clear implications for match sharpness and conditioning, which is why clubs will often reintroduce players gradually. A friendly against an elite opponent like Juventus offers a controlled environment for such a reintroduction while also providing a stern test for any player returning to action.

Analysis

This squad inclusion should be viewed as an initial step rather than a definitive return to competitive action. Coaches often use friendlies to manage minutes carefully, and being part of a travelling party implies a level of readiness but not necessarily a full match return. From a squad-management perspective, reintroducing a player after a prolonged suspension involves balancing fitness, team chemistry, and public relations. For the player, an appearance — even as a substitute in a friendly — could be instrumental in rebuilding match awareness and confidence, while for the club it offers a way to monitor progress without immediate competitive pressure.

BBC Sport's report that Mykhailo Mudryk has been included in Chelsea's squad for the Hong Kong friendly with Juventus hints at a potential first step back after a long absence. Whether he takes to the field will depend on the coaching staff's assessment, but the selection itself is a notable development in his road to match action.
