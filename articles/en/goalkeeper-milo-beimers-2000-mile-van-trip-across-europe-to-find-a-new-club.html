Goalkeeper Milo Beimers' 2,000-mile van trip across Europe to find a new club

8/11/2026 · By Koora Live

Goalkeeper Milo Beimers has embarked on a 2,000-mile journey across Europe with a single objective: to secure a new club while operating from the back of a van. BBC Sport reported the unusual approach, highlighting the player’s decision to travel long-distance and use his van as a base while seeking opportunities.

The basics of the trip

According to BBC Sport, Beimers is undertaking a 2,000-mile route across Europe and is based in the back of a van while he looks for a new team. That description alone underlines the scale of the undertaking: long-distance travel, repeated movement between locations and the practical challenge of living and preparing for training and trial opportunities on the road. The decision to use a vehicle as a mobile base reflects an itinerant approach to finding professional opportunities that requires careful planning and resilience.

Living out of a van while pursuing trials or short-term training stints raises immediate logistical questions. Players in this situation must manage day-to-day needs — sleep, recovery, training sessions, and communications with clubs and agents — while navigating travel schedules and varying time zones and climates across different parts of Europe. Although details of Beimers’ exact itinerary and contacts are not provided beyond the BBC Sport report, the picture is one of a player taking a proactive, unconventional route to remain visible to scouts and clubs.

Why players pursue unconventional routes to sign for clubs

Being out of contract is a difficult position for any professional footballer. Many players who are between clubs explore a variety of routes to get back into the game: scheduled trials with clubs, guest appearances in training sessions, attending open trials, working with an agent to set up tryouts, or leveraging video and social media content to attract interest. Using mobility — whether through intensive travel or temporary bases — is one way to increase the number of opportunities a player can access within a limited window.

There is a long history in football of players taking non-linear paths to (re)establish their careers. Lower-league and non-league systems across Europe are interconnected with more prominent leagues, and a strong performance at a trial or a short-term spell can reopen doors. From a practical perspective, the route Beimers has chosen emphasizes readiness to seize local opportunities: being nearby makes it easier to accept last-minute invites to train or trial, and a self-contained base like a van reduces accommodation costs while keeping the player flexible.

Analysis

This story, as reported by BBC Sport, points to broader themes in the modern game: competition for contracts is fierce and players sometimes need to be entrepreneurial and highly mobile to generate opportunities. Analysts might see such a journey as a pragmatic response to market realities — a combination of determination, self-promotion and willingness to accept the hardships that come with an unconventional search. At the same time, clubs weigh short-term trials against long-term planning; a player’s availability and readiness must be balanced with the club’s immediate needs and medical assurances.

Milo Beimers’ decision to cover 2,000 miles across Europe from a van base — as detailed by BBC Sport — is an extreme but telling example of the lengths some players will go to stay in the game. Whether that commitment pays off will depend on opportunities, timing and the responses of clubs along the route.
