FPL tips from last season's winner: what Erik Ibsen's BBC Sport feature means for your mini‑league

8/19/2026 · By Koora Live

BBC Sport recently ran a feature in which Erik Ibsen — described in the piece as last season's Fantasy Premier League (FPL) champion — sets out his five best tips for managers wanting to win mini‑leagues. The brief excerpt presents Ibsen as an experienced successful manager who is sharing practical advice for other players.

This article unpacks why a champion's perspective matters, explains core FPL principles that commonly underpin winning campaigns, and offers guidance on how to adapt such high‑level tips to your own mini‑league strategy. Where relevant we point readers back to the BBC Sport piece for Ibsen's original words and tips.

Why advice from a reigning champion is useful

Winning a Fantasy Premier League competition requires more than luck: consistent decision‑making across 38 gameweeks, an ability to react to injuries and fixture swings, and a good sense of when to take calculated risks. That combination of skills is why insight from a manager who secured first place in a competition carries weight for other players.

BBC Sport's short feature highlights that Erik Ibsen reached the top of the global FPL leaderboard last season and distilled his experience into five top tips. While this article does not reproduce those tips verbatim, the provenance — a champion sharing practical lessons — is what makes the feature worth attention for anyone serious about improving their mini‑league performance.

Core FPL principles to pair with champion advice

There are well‑established, game‑wide strategies that tend to form the backbone of successful FPL seasons. These include managing your budget to buy in-form players, planning transfers around fixture difficulty, using captaincy to maximise upside, and deploying chips (wildcard, free hit, bench boost, triple captain) at moments when they can have the greatest effect. These mechanics are part of the official Fantasy Premier League game structure and are widely accepted as the primary levers managers can control.

Another consistent consideration is risk management. Overcommitting to speculative differentials can pay off, but it also increases volatility. Conversely, following mainstream ownership and reliable starters reduces variance but may make it harder to climb leaderboards rapidly. Champions often balance these approaches — protecting against season‑ruining errors while picking moments to swing for extra points. Adapting that balance to your mini‑league's dynamics (how conservative or aggressive your rivals are) is a crucial step when applying any external advice.

Analysis

Erik Ibsen’s willingness to share five tips via BBC Sport signals a broader trend in fantasy football: top performers often combine technical game knowledge with psychological discipline. From managing transfers to timing chips, champions usually excel at planning ahead while remaining flexible when the unexpected happens. Readers should view Ibsen’s pointers as a starting point — evidence from a proven manager — rather than a guaranteed formula.

Applying champion advice effectively means translating high‑level principles into specific weekly actions. Consider your mini‑league rivals' tendencies, your tolerance for risk, and your existing squad composition before copying any single approach wholesale. In short, champion insight is valuable, but context and execution determine whether that advice helps you climb the table.

BBC Sport’s brief profile of Erik Ibsen — last season’s FPL champion — and his five best tips is a timely reminder that studying the habits of successful managers can sharpen your own approach. Combine those champion insights with sound, widely accepted FPL practices and tailor them to your mini‑league’s unique dynamics to give yourself the best chance of topping the table.
