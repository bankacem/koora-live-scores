Premier League shirts gambling ban: how kits will change after clubs vote

8/13/2026 · By Koora Live

Premier League kits are set for a striking visual shift after clubs voted to prohibit gambling companies from appearing on the front of shirts. BBC Sport reports that this decision will alter the look of shirts this season and explores the history of sponsorship and who might step into the vacuum left on prime kit real estate.

A brief history of shirt sponsorship in English football

Commercial branding on football shirts has been a defining feature of the modern game for decades. What began as a way for clubs to secure additional revenue has evolved into a major component of club identity and global marketing. Over the last generation, a wide range of industries — from airlines and automotive firms to technology and financial services — have used front-of-shirt sponsorship as a way to reach large, passionate audiences.

In recent years, gambling firms became particularly visible in English football, investing heavily to front shirts and stadiums. That prominence made them a familiar sight on matchdays and in broadcasts around the world. The clubs' recent vote to ban gambling on the front of shirts is therefore a major commercial and visual shift, changing one of football’s most recognisable canvases.

What the ban means for clubs, designs and commercial strategy

Removing gambling logos from the shirt front will force clubs and kit partners to rethink design, revenue and marketing plans. The front of the shirt is the most valuable advertising position; replacing that income stream will require clubs to secure new partners or pursue alternative commercial arrangements. Kit manufacturers and creative directors will also be tasked with producing fresh looks that reflect new sponsors or, in some cases, showcase unbranded or charitable messaging.

Fans can expect a mixture of responses. Some clubs may pursue different types of corporate partners — such as firms in technology, retail, consumer goods or finance — while others might use the space for charitable or club-related themes, temporary artwork, or increased emphasis on sleeve and training sponsorships. From a matchday perspective, broadcasters and retailers will also adapt to the changed visual landscape as new partnerships are rolled out.

Analysis

This development suggests a period of rapid commercial adjustment for Premier League clubs. While the front-of-shirt position has been lucrative, clubs have commercial teams and global brands that can respond quickly; analysts might see this as an opportunity for clubs to broaden their corporate partnerships and align with sponsors that reflect changing public sentiment. From a design standpoint, kit makers will be under pressure to produce distinctive shirts that make up for the loss of a high-visibility brand while keeping supporters engaged.

The decision by clubs to remove gambling firms from the front of Premier League shirts represents a notable change in the commercial and visual fabric of the competition. As BBC Sport outlines, the coming season will show how clubs, sponsors and kit makers adapt to a redesigned kit landscape.

Frequently Asked Questions

What exactly did the clubs vote to do?

Clubs voted to ban gambling companies from appearing on the front of shirts, a change that BBC Sport says will make Premier League kits look very different this season.

Does the ban mean no gambling advertising in the Premier League at all?

The information provided notes a ban on gambling branding specifically on the front of shirts. It does not detail other advertising placements such as sleeves, back-of-shirt, stadium signage or digital advertising, so those areas were not confirmed in the report.

Who will replace gambling sponsors on shirts?

BBC Sport examines who might replace gambling sponsors, but the vote itself does not name replacements. In general, clubs typically pursue a range of alternatives including other corporate sectors, charitable partnerships, or different commercial arrangements to fill prime kit positions.
