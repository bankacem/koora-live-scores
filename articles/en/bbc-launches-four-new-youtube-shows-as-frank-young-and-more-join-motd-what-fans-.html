BBC launches four new YouTube shows as Frank, Young and more join MOTD — what fans need to know

8/19/2026 · By Koora Live

BBC Sport has announced a digital expansion that will bring Match of the Day (MOTD) content into new formats, with a headline naming “Frank, Young and more” as joining MOTD and the broadcaster launching four new YouTube shows. According to BBC Sport, the move is part of a wider strategy to bring football fans closer to the action across TV, radio, YouTube, the BBC website and the BBC Sport app.

What the announcement means for Match of the Day

Match of the Day is the BBC’s long‑running football highlights brand and a principal outlet for domestic match coverage. The BBC Sport headline makes clear that new contributors described as “Frank, Young and more” will be associated with MOTD as the corporation broadens its content offering. While the headline does not supply full names or roles, the association of new personalities with MOTD signals an intent to refresh the programme’s on‑screen and online presence.

Historically, MOTD has combined match highlights, expert analysis and studio discussion to capture the weekend’s key moments in domestic football. Bringing additional contributors into the MOTD fold, alongside new YouTube content, suggests the BBC is looking to expand both the depth and the formats through which supporters can engage with analysis and reaction.

A multi‑platform approach: TV, radio, YouTube, website and app

BBC Sport explicitly frames this initiative as a cross‑platform push. The broadcaster says it will bring fans closer to the action across traditional and digital channels — television and radio, as well as YouTube, the BBC website and the BBC Sport app. This kind of integrated distribution recognises how modern audiences consume sport: live matches and highlight shows remain central, but social and streaming platforms are increasingly important for clips, interviews and short‑form analysis.

The decision to launch four new shows on YouTube complements the existing offerings on broadcast and digital BBC platforms. YouTube is commonly used by broadcasters to reach younger and international viewers, provide shareable clips and host more experimental or personality‑driven formats that sit alongside flagship broadcast programmes.

Analysis

This move can be seen as the BBC adapting a traditional, trusted football brand to the habits of a fragmented audience. Launching multiple YouTube shows while reinforcing presence across TV, radio, the website and app allows the corporation to offer both long‑form, appointment‑to‑view content and on‑demand, bite‑sized material. Analysts might view the decision as an attempt to retain existing viewers who expect comprehensive, curated football coverage while also reaching younger users who increasingly rely on social and streaming platforms for highlights and punditry.

The BBC’s announcement, reported by BBC Sport, signals a notable expansion of MOTD’s footprint into YouTube with four new shows and new contributors named in the headline as “Frank, Young and more.” Framed as part of a wider effort to connect with audiences across TV, radio, website and app, the initiative highlights how legacy sports brands are evolving to meet changing viewing habits.
