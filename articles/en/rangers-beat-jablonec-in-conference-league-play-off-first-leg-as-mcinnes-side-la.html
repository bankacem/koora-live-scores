Rangers beat Jablonec in Conference League play-off first leg as McInnes' side labour

8/20/2026 · By Koora Live

Rangers secured a win over Czech side Jablonec in the first leg of their UEFA Europa Conference League play-off, but it was far from a fluent performance, according to a match report by Tom English for BBC Sport. The victory gives Rangers a foothold in the tie, yet the manner of the performance left plenty for supporters and pundits to debate.

A win that felt hard-won

BBC Sport's Tom English described the encounter as a display that lacked sparkle, with Rangers getting the job done without producing their most convincing football. Reporting on the first leg of the Conference League play-off, the coverage makes clear the outcome favoured the Scottish side, but also stresses the match was not a comfortable or aesthetically pleasing performance.

For followers of European qualifiers, first legs can often be cagey affairs as teams balance attack and caution. That context helps explain why even a winning performance might be described as laboured: securing a positive result while managing risk is frequently the priority in these two-legged ties.

Context: what the result means and why the manner of victory matters

In UEFA qualifying rounds such as the Conference League play-off, the first leg is a crucial step toward reaching the group stage. Rangers’ advantage after this opening match gives them a platform ahead of the return fixture, but the tone of the game — reportedly subdued and unconvincing — is likely to be a talking point for manager McInnes and the dressing room.

Analysts might see the performance as a warning sign if Rangers hope to progress deep into European competition; conversely, pragmatists will argue that winning away or at home in a play-off first leg, regardless of style, is the essential objective. These competing readings underline why both result and performance are examined closely after ties like this one.

Analysis

Framing this as a piece of analysis: the report’s emphasis on a lacklustre showing suggests that, while results remain the immediate priority in two-legged European ties, style and control are not irrelevant. Teams that rely too heavily on narrow wins without convincing performances can become vulnerable in the return leg. Given the balance between cautious strategy and the need to assert dominance over two matches, McInnes' side will likely focus on sharpening their attacking cohesion and defensive compactness before the second game.

From a broader perspective, this match is a reminder of the ruthlessness of European qualifying: clubs from different leagues bring contrasting approaches and the margin for error can be small. Supporters and commentators will weigh the positive of a first-leg victory against the negative of an uninspired performance — a debate that can shape the mood around the tie heading into the decisive second leg.

The first leg gave Rangers the all-important lead in their Conference League play-off, but as BBC Sport’s Tom English noted, the performance left questions to be answered. With the tie far from concluded, the return fixture will be an opportunity for McInnes’ side to both protect their advantage and demonstrate a stronger, more assured level of play.
