Michael Carrick urges Manchester United to make more signings ahead of first full season

8/13/2026 · By Koora Live

Michael Carrick has told reporters that Manchester United need additional players as he prepares for his first full season in charge, according to BBC Sport. In an interview published by the broadcaster, Carrick outlined why strengthening the squad is necessary, reflected on his own position within the club and discussed the roles of Marcus Rashford and Kobbie Mainoo as United plan their next move in the transfer market.

Why Carrick believes more signings are needed

In the BBC Sport piece, Carrick set out a clear message: the squad requires reinforcements ahead of the coming campaign. That call mirrors a familiar line from managers across elite leagues — squad depth and quality are essential to sustain a challenge across multiple competitions. Carrick’s argument, as reported, centres on building a group that can cope with injuries, fixture congestion and the tactical demands of modern football.

Manchester United, as a high-profile club with regular participation in domestic and European competitions, traditionally looks to add players in key areas during transfer windows. Carrick’s public recognition of that need signals that the coach and the club’s recruitment staff will be engaged in discussions about targets and priorities as they attempt to assemble a competitive squad for the season ahead.

Rashford, Kobbie Mainoo and Carrick’s position at the club

BBC Sport notes that Carrick spoke about Marcus Rashford and Kobbie Mainoo as part of his assessment of the squad. Marcus Rashford is one of United’s most prominent attacking players, while Kobbie Mainoo has emerged from the club’s youth system as a promising midfield prospect. The inclusion of both names in Carrick’s comments highlights a mix of established senior talent and developing academy graduates that managers often balance when planning a season.

Carrick also reflected on being in what he described as a privileged position at the club. As a former Manchester United player who has moved into coaching and then first-team management, that perspective is shaped by an intimate knowledge of the club’s culture and resources. Being 'privileged' can refer to having access to top-level facilities, a talented player pool and support structures — all of which influence how a coach approaches recruitment, training and squad management.

Analysis

Carrick’s public emphasis on recruitment is significant because incoming players shape not only tactical options but also the club’s long-term trajectory. Managers at clubs with Manchester United’s stature generally operate under pressure to deliver immediate results while also integrating younger talents into the first team. Carrick’s comments, as reported by BBC Sport, suggest he is balancing those demands: acknowledging existing strengths — such as established stars and promising academy graduates — while also pressing for further investments to address shortfalls.

For supporters and observers, the practical question becomes which positions the club prioritises and how recruitment will align with Carrick’s vision. Transfers are influenced by budgetary realities, scouting networks and market opportunity, and Carrick’s public stance could be intended to ensure clarity about the need for reinforcements as discussions progress behind the scenes.

Carrick’s interview with BBC Sport sets a clear early tone for his tenure: he wants reinforcements while respecting the blend of experienced players and academy prospects already at the club. How Manchester United respond in the transfer market will be watched closely as the coach prepares for his first full season.
