Irish FA Withdraws Support for Gianni Infantino's Fifa Re-Election Bid

8/25/2026 · By Koora Live

The Irish Football Association (IFA) has taken a decisive stance ahead of next year's Fifa presidential elections, announcing that it will not support Gianni Infantino’s bid for re-election. In a clear statement calling for change within football’s global governing body, the IFA has set the tone for a potentially divisive election cycle.

Irish FA Calls for Change at Fifa Leadership

The Irish Football Association has voiced its dissatisfaction with the current state of Fifa leadership under Gianni Infantino. In a public declaration stating it is "time for change," the IFA confirmed it will not back Infantino in his bid for re-election as Fifa president in 2024. This decision highlights mounting calls by sections of the footballing community for reform and fresh leadership within the global governing body of the sport.

Gianni Infantino's Controversial Tenure at Fifa

Gianni Infantino has been at the helm of Fifa since 2016, succeeding Sepp Blatter amidst a series of corruption scandals that deeply tarnished the organization's reputation. His tenure has been characterized by ambitious initiatives, including the expansion of the FIFA World Cup to 48 teams and discussions around more frequent iterations of the tournament. However, his leadership has also drawn criticism from various quarters for a lack of transparency and accountability. The Irish FA’s decision to withdraw support aligns with voices advocating for greater openness and structural adjustments within Fifa.

Analysis

The Irish FA’s move is significant as it signals growing discontent with Infantino's leadership, even among traditionally less outspoken associations. While the detailed reasoning behind their decision remains unclear, the explicit mention of the need for 'change' hints at deeper dissatisfaction potentially shared by other federations. This development could lead to a more contested Fifa election, as opposition to Infantino’s presidency gathers momentum. The statement is also noteworthy within the broader context of Fifa's historical resistance to change, demonstrating that national associations may be seeking a shift away from the status quo.

The Irish FA's withdrawal of support for Gianni Infantino's re-election marks a notable shift in the dynamic surrounding Fifa's leadership. As the 2024 presidential election approaches, this declaration could inspire wider debate about the future direction of football’s governing body globally.
