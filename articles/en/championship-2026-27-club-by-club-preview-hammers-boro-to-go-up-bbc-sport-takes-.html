BBC Sport has published a club-by-club preview examining the 24 teams taking part in the 2026-27 Championship season. The outlet’s headline — asking whether the “Hammers & Boro” will go up — frames the broader conversation around promotion and which clubs might challenge over the long second tier campaign.

What a club-by-club preview aims to deliver

A club-by-club preview is designed to give readers context on every side in a 24-team competition: strengths and weaknesses, tactical tendencies, squad depth, and where each team might realistically finish. BBC Sport’s correspondents have undertaken that task for the 2026-27 Championship season, producing a snapshot of the field that fans and neutral observers can use to frame their expectations.

These previews typically consider managerial stability, recent transfer activity, injury risks and any structural advantages such as financial backing or an especially strong academy. For a competition as long and gruelling as the Championship, where teams play 46 league matches across the season, those factors can be decisive and are the sorts of details broadcasters and analysts emphasise in advance pieces.

Readers should treat such previews as informed assessments rather than definitive forecasts: they summarise what is known at a point in time and highlight storylines to follow once the season starts. BBC Sport’s approach — assigning correspondents to look closely at each club — is a standard way to map out the narrative landscape of a campaign that often produces surprises.

Why the headline names — ‘Hammers’ and ‘Boro’ — matter

The BBC Sport headline mentions the ‘Hammers’ and ‘Boro’, shorthand nicknames widely recognised in English football. ‘Hammers’ is a common moniker for West Ham United and ‘Boro’ is frequently used for Middlesbrough; using nicknames in a headline is a way to signal familiar, high-profile storylines to readers.

That phrasing also highlights promotion as the central question: in any Championship preview, attention gravitates to who might secure automatic promotion, who will contest the play-offs, and which clubs could face a relegation battle. The Championship’s format—24 teams, each playing 46 league games—means endurance, squad rotation and mid-season adjustments often determine final placings, so naming eye-catching clubs in a headline helps focus debate.

Framing the discussion around known club brands or nicknames is useful editorially because it combines recognisable identifiers with a provocative question. BBC Sport’s choice indicates the correspondents are highlighting those clubs as part of the season’s narrative without asserting a fixed outcome.
