FPL guide: Is Haaland worth the price and should you pick Rogers or Palmer?

8/18/2026 · By Koora Live

BBC Sport's Fantasy Premier League panel has been working through some of the headline debates managers face ahead of the new season — from whether Erling Haaland justifies his price tag to the dilemma of choosing between Rogers or Palmer. With the FPL deadline looming on Friday, their discussion is designed to help managers finalise squads and make informed opening-week decisions.

This piece distils the themes from BBC Sport's experts and adds practical, general guidance for FPL managers. It avoids proclaiming any single 'correct' move — instead it sets out the factors to weigh when building a balanced squad, managing risk and aiming for strong early-season returns.

Weighing up Haaland: price versus premium return

Erling Haaland is one of the game’s most recognised goal-scorers and a headline name in many FPL conversations. BBC Sport’s team posed the central question: is he worth the price? That framing captures the trade-off every manager faces when considering an expensive forward — committing a large portion of your budget to a single player reduces flexibility elsewhere but can also deliver outsized returns if the player performs as expected.

When deciding whether to carry a premium asset such as Haaland, managers typically weigh several general factors: the player’s historical scoring rate and involvement in goals, the strength and consistency of his club’s attack, early-season fixture difficulty, and the opportunity cost of not selecting alternative attackers or reinvesting funds into midfield or defence. Captaincy potential is another key consideration: a premium forward who is a reliable captain option most weeks increases his appeal.

For many teams, the choice is less binary than it looks. Some managers will allocate funds to a premium striker and balance the squad with budget enablers elsewhere; others prefer to spread value across several mid-priced players to reduce variance. BBC Sport’s experts highlight that there is no universal solution — the best pick depends on your strategy, tolerance for risk and how you plan to use chips and transfers later in the season.

The Rogers vs Palmer dilemma and squad-construction principles

Picking between players such as Rogers or Palmer — another of the season’s debated options raised by BBC Sport — illustrates the micro-decisions FPL managers must make. Rather than relying on headlines, successful choices usually focus on predictable minutes, role in the team (starter versus rotation risk), set-piece duty and favourable fixtures.

Beyond individual comparisons, broader squad-building rules remain valuable. Balance is essential: aim to combine reliable starters with a handful of higher-upside differentials. Consider fixture runs and how they align with your transfer plans: having several players with attractive early schedules can boost immediate returns. Also think about bench structure — a couple of playing substitutes can save points in case of rotation or unexpected absences.

Finally, remember that early deadlines and pre-season moves can change the landscape quickly. BBC Sport’s discussion explicitly aimed to help managers ahead of Friday’s deadline, and that timing means weighing short-term opportunities against longer-term flexibility. For those unsure, phased approaches — such as delaying a high-cost purchase until you see actual minutes in competitive matches — remain a prudent option.

Analysis

BBC Sport’s panel framed the core FPL dilemmas in practical terms rather than offering definitive prescriptions. That approach reflects the reality of the game: personal strategy and risk appetite drive many choices. Managers who favour stability may accept the reduced flexibility of a premium-name if he increases captaincy certainty. Conversely, those who prize adaptability might spread funds more evenly to cope with early-season surprises.

In short, the right decision usually aligns with an explicit plan. Decide whether you want early explosive upside, steady differential accumulation, or tactical flexibility for the transfer market and select players who fit that blueprint. BBC Sport’s experts emphasise clarifying your objective before the Friday deadline so your first transfers and captain choices support a coherent strategy.
