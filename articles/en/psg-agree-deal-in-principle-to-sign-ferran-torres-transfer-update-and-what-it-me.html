PSG agree deal in principle to sign Ferran Torres — transfer update and what it means

8/13/2026 · By Koora Live

BBC Sport reports that Paris St-Germain have agreed a deal in principle to sign Barcelona forward Ferran Torres from Barcelona for about £43m. The headline capture confirms the clubs have reached an understanding over a transfer that, if completed, would see the Spain international move from one European heavyweight to another.

The headline facts: what BBC Sport has revealed

According to BBC Sport, Paris St-Germain and Barcelona have agreed a deal in principle for Ferran Torres for approximately £43m. That central piece of information is the only confirmed detail of this specific transaction reported in the source: the destination (PSG), the selling club (Barcelona), the player (Ferran Torres) and the approximate fee are all stated by the outlet.

A "deal in principle" typically indicates that clubs have reached consensus on the main financial terms, but further formalities remain before completion. BBC Sport's report is the basis for this update and should be treated as the confirmed public report on the matter until either club issues an official announcement or further reporting provides additional specifics.

Background on the clubs and the player involved

Paris St-Germain are one of French football's dominant clubs, playing home matches at the Parc des Princes and competing regularly for domestic titles and in European competition. Barcelona are an established giant of Spanish and European football, traditionally based at the Camp Nou and known for producing and attracting top attacking talent.

Ferran Torres is a Spain international who operates principally as a forward. As a player profile, he is recognised for versatility across the front line and has experience in top-tier leagues. It is general, well-established context that Torres previously featured at Valencia and Manchester City before his spell with Barcelona. These elements form the broader footballing context around the move reported by BBC Sport.
