Chelsea split training squads at Cobham, hinting at possible departures before window closes

8/19/2026 · By Koora Live

Chelsea’s recent reshuffle of its training groups at the Cobham training centre has attracted headlines, with the Premier League club placing a number of its players into separate academies. The move, reported by BBC Sport, places several individuals who may be on the brink of a transfer before the next window shuts. Among those affected are midfielder Liam Delap, defender Nicolas Jackson, and Ukrainian fullback Mykhailo Mudryk.

The split comes at a time when clubs with deep squads are looking for new bargains or lining up exits to reduce wage bills and create space for younger talent. By segregating players, Chelsea offers both a clearer focus on development for the retained group and an environment that might ease the transition for those planning to leave.

Cobham’s Dual‑Group Setup Signals Pre‑Window Moves

Established in 1938, Chelsea’s E4 training base at Cobham has long been a hub for the club’s footballing operations. The recent decision to host two discrete training groups suggests that the coaching staff are juggling priorities: keeping the main squad sharp while concurrently working with a subset of players who may be earmarked for the next transfer window.

The high‑profile names attached to this segregation include the centre‑midfielder Liam Delap, who recently finished a loan spell with Bolton and won a trophy there, as well as Nicolas Jackson, an academy product who has raced through the ranks to sign a professional contract. Mykhailo Mudryk, a Ukrainian fullback who joined Chelsea in 2022, also appears to be in this emergent cohort.

The separation is not arbitrary; it allows the coaching team to tailor training intensity and match exposure to the individual readiness of each player. For those likely to depart, a lighter physical workload combined with targeted match fitness can speed the handover process. Conversely, first‑team regulars can continue to hone tactical cohesion within a tightly knit unit.

From a wider footballing perspective, this approach aligns with other Premier League clubs that use training splits to manage squad depth during congested periods, especially as the transfer window home stretch draws near. The timing, positions, and jersey numbers involved hint at strategic hand‑off, but the club has not confirmed any definitive list of exits.

In this context, the re‑evaluation of player roles at Chelsea could signal a shift toward a leaner roster, prioritising young talent and newly recruited signings for the next season.

Number 9 No Longer a Chelsea Shirt: Delap’s Changing Role

One forward‑looking detail that caught attention is Liam Delap’s loss of the number 9 shirt — a number often associated with a club’s leading goal‑scorer. While the club hasn’t issued an official statement, the change typically indicates a possible transition in squad hierarchy or an imminent move.

Delap’s time with Chelsea began in their academy, and the midfielder has journeyed through numerous loan spells across the Championship and League One before landing a short league stint in the Premier League. He played a part in helping Bolton Wanderers secure promotion last season, a feat that underscores his match‑winning ability.

Regardless of his shirt number, Delap’s inclusion in the split training group suggests he may no longer feature prominently in the first team's plans. For players with a number that signals senior status, the reassignment can carry a psychological weight, often correlating with reduced playing time or impending transfer.

From a tactical point of view, Chelsea’s decision to reassign these players far from the first‑team environment could be part‑of a broader strategy to give younger squad members more space to thrive while smoothing out a potential roster turnover. The club has historically been known for rotating successes, and such moves sometimes precipitate a renewal cycle where emerging generations take a step down the pecking order.

Whether or not a permanent deal surfaces, Delap’s trajectory illustrates the fluid nature of modern football squads, where market dynamics, positional demands, and club philosophies continuously reshape player pathways.

Analysis

BBC Sport’s report points to a clear signalling of Chelsea’s intent to manage its roster by segregating those on the brink of departure from the core first‑team train‑ing ‘pulse’. This process offers a potential two‑fold benefit: it eases the psychological stress of contingent players while preserving the intensity of the main squad’s pre‑season preparations. Analysts might interpret this move as a strategic pause, with the club weighing the necessity of swapping experienced disposables for youthful dynamism, especially as the transfer window’s deadline looms. If transfer rumours trend, the clear delineation into groups will likely become a focal point for supporters, underscoring the club’s adaptive approach to the modern era of football economics and talent pipeline management.

Chelsea’s re‑organisation of its training structure at Cobham reveals the club’s proactive approach to managing its squad ahead of a hectic transfer season. By distinguishing between those likely to stay and those open to new adventures, the Blues position themselves for a potentially smoother transition, maintaining continuity among first‑team core players while facilitating the movement of others as the market closes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why has Chelsea split its training groups?

The club’s decision, documented by BBC Sport, aims to separate players who may be on the cusp of a move from those likely to stay in the first team, allowing both groups to focus on their respective development paths.

What could this mean for Liam Delap?

Delap’s placement in the secondary training squad and the loss of the number 9 shirt suggest the club may be considering a move for him before the transfer window closes, although no official confirmation has been given.
