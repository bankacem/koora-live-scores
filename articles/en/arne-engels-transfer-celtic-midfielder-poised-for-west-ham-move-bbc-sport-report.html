How the medical fits into the transfer process

A medical examination is a standard element of professional football transfers. It typically takes place once clubs and the player have agreed on personal terms and any fee, and before the deal is officially registered. The medical enables the buying club's medical team to assess a player's physical condition and identify any issues that could affect the contract or future availability. If the player clears the tests and both clubs complete necessary paperwork, the transfer can then be formally announced and registered with the relevant league and governing bodies.

Analysis

From a sporting perspective, a transfer reaching the medical stage usually signals serious intent from the buying club and indicates that negotiations over terms have been productive. For Celtic, the loss of a midfielder can require tactical adjustments or reliance on squad depth — common challenges when a regular first-team player departs. For West Ham United, completing the move would fit the familiar pattern of Premier League clubs reinforcing their squads through incoming signings. Analysts might see the involvement of a medical as the penultimate step before registration and announcement, but it is important to remember that transfers only become official once all parties sign the necessary contracts and governing bodies complete registration.

The update from BBC Sport, backed by confirmation from Martin O'Neill, places Arne Engels' prospective transfer to West Ham United at a decisive stage. While the medical is a strong indicator that the clubs and player are close to finalising terms, the move remains officially pending until the final formalities are completed and announced.
