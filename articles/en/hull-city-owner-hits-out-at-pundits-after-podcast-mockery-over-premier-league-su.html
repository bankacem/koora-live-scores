Hull City owner hits out at pundits after podcast mockery over Premier League survival

8/20/2026 · By Koora Live

Hull City owner Acun Ilicali has publicly criticised pundits Joe Cole and Carlton Cole after the pair mocked his club's chances of staying in the Premier League on a podcast, according to BBC Sport. The exchange highlights growing tensions between club leadership and media voices as the business and sporting sides of top‑flight football collide.

What happened: a podcast row draws a reaction

According to BBC Sport, Acun Ilicali has taken aim at former players-turned-pundits Joe Cole and Carlton Cole after they mocked Hull City's prospects of Premier League survival on a podcast. The precise remarks made on the podcast are not detailed here; the reported fact is that the pundits mocked the club's chances and that Ilicali criticised them in response. Podcasts have become an influential platform for former players to offer analysis and opinion, and that reach means comments can quickly draw a response from those they concern.

Media scrutiny is a familiar element of modern football, and club owners increasingly find themselves responding to pundit commentary that they consider unfair or damaging. In this instance, the owner of a Premier League club chose to publicly register his displeasure with two well-known media figures, illustrating how off-field narratives can take on significance during a club's season.

The personalities involved and the wider context

Acun Ilicali is identified as the owner of Hull City. Joe Cole and Carlton Cole are both former professional players who have built profiles in broadcast and podcasting, providing analysis and opinion on the game. Joe Cole, a former England international midfielder, and Carlton Cole, a former striker, are recognised figures in UK football media.

The Premier League is the highest level of English club football and survival in the division is a central focus for clubs fighting relegation. Pundits' assessments of a team's likelihood to remain in the league can influence public perception among supporters, sponsors and other stakeholders. Given that environment, it is perhaps unsurprising that a club owner might challenge media commentary he views as dismissive or unjust.

Analysis

This episode underscores the porous boundary between media opinion and club management in modern football. Punditry — particularly on podcasts, where conversation can be candid and wide-ranging — often mixes entertainment with analysis. Club owners, who are accountable to supporters and financial stakeholders, may see a duty to push back when commentary is interpreted as undermining morale or commercial standing. Analysts might view Ilicali's response as an attempt to protect his club's image and assert authority over the narrative surrounding Hull City's season. Alternatively, commentators may argue that robust debate is part of the sport's public life. Either way, the clash highlights how every public comment can become part of the broader story of a club's campaign.

The episode reported by BBC Sport is a reminder that media commentary and club leadership often intersect in high-stakes ways during a Premier League season. As podcasts and pundit platforms continue to grow in influence, similar public exchanges between owners and commentators are likely to remain part of the sport's landscape.
