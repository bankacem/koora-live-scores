Why Ander Herrera is Playing for Free in Spain's Third Division

8/27/2026 · By Koora Live

Ander Herrera, a name synonymous with elite European football, has taken an unexpected turn in his illustrious career. The former Manchester United and Paris Saint-Germain midfielder is now playing without a salary in Spain's third division, a stark contrast to his time at top-tier clubs. BBC Sport dives into the reasons behind this intriguing decision.

A Career Spent at Football's Summit

Ander Herrera has built a reputation as a dependable and hardworking midfielder, spending the prime of his career in the upper echelons of European football. He developed his skills at Real Zaragoza before making his mark at Athletic Club, where his performances earned him a high-profile move to Manchester United in 2014. At Old Trafford, Herrera became a fan favorite due to his tenacity and reliability, winning an FA Cup, an EFL Cup, and the UEFA Europa League. He later joined Paris Saint-Germain in 2019, further bolstering his résumé with Ligue 1 titles and experience in the UEFA Champions League. Few would have predicted that a player of his pedigree would now be plying his trade in the Spanish third division.

Coming Home and Giving Back

The story of Herrera's return to Spanish football is one of personal values and emotional ties rather than financial gain. While the specifics of this move are not detailed by BBC Sport, it is clear that the decision to play for free highlights his love for the game and desire to contribute to a local cause. Such a choice underscores his commitment to football beyond monetary rewards, potentially reflecting his connection to his roots and the community that shaped his career.

Analysis

Herrera's decision to step away from the financial rewards of elite football for an unpaid role in Spain's third tier signals the profound influence of personal motivation in the sport. At a time when footballers are often scrutinized for exorbitant wages and glamorous lifestyles, Herrera’s move could be seen as a refreshing counter-narrative. It might also indicate his desire to lead by example, inspiring young players and demonstrating that loyalty and passion can sometimes outweigh financial considerations. Such actions could rekindle discussions about the role of community and identity in modern football, particularly in an era dominated by commercial aspirations.

Ander Herrera's decision to play in Spain's third division without a salary is a powerful reminder of football's emotional and community-driven dimensions. His story challenges the norm and serves as an inspiration in a sport often defined by its commercial aspects.
