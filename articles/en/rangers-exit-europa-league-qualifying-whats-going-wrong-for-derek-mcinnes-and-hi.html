Rangers exit Europa League qualifying — what's going wrong for Derek McInnes and his side?

8/14/2026 · By Koora Live

BBC Sport has asked a blunt question after Rangers' Europa League qualifying defeat by Jagellonia Bialystok: what is going wrong for new manager Derek McInnes and his team so far this season? That defeat has put fresh scrutiny on an early phase of McInnes's tenure and raised broader questions about adjustment, standards and expectations at one of Scottish football's biggest clubs.

Immediate fallout: why a European qualifying exit matters

A knockout in Europa League qualifying is notable for any club that sees European competition as part of its competitive and commercial landscape. For Rangers, a club with a long history in domestic and continental football, elimination at this stage affects more than just next month's fixtures: it impacts momentum, revenue projections and the message sent to supporters. BBC Sport's coverage framed the result as a catalyst for debate about the squad and the manager, highlighting how quickly pressure can mount when early-season targets are missed.

European qualifiers also expose transitional issues. New managers commonly need time to imprint ideas, adapt training methods and fine-tune player roles, but two-legged or single-match knockout formats leave little room for gradual progress. The timing of a European exit amplifies the spotlight on those early processes, and supporters and media naturally look for explanations when a club fails to advance.

Areas to scrutinise without jumping to conclusions

When assessing what might be going wrong, it helps to break the situation down into manageable areas: tactical clarity, player recruitment and cohesion, fitness and mental sharpness, and the wider club context. Tactical change under a new manager can produce short-term inconsistency as players adapt to different responsibilities. Recruitment cycles often overlap with managerial appointments, which can lead to mismatches between the squad inherited and the profile the incoming coach prefers.

Fitness, preparation and sharpness are other common factors early in a season. Even accomplished squads can be vulnerable in knockout fixtures if they lack match rhythm or collective understanding. Finally, the off-field environment — expectations from the board, media scrutiny and supporter sentiment — can shape how a team responds after a disappointing result. None of these areas is unique to any single club, but combined they create the conditions in which a European exit becomes a measuring stick for new management.

Analysis

Drawing on wider patterns in the game, this situation suggests that a single result — however unwelcome — should be examined in the context of managerial transition and squad construction. Analysts might see the elimination as an early signal that adjustments are required, but it is also true that many successful managers have weathered early setbacks while building a coherent project. The key indicators to monitor in the weeks that follow will be how quickly the team shows improved understanding of tactical instructions, whether the squad is tweaked to better fit the manager's model, and how resilience is demonstrated in subsequent matches.

BBC Sport's piece highlights how a single European setback can sharpen questions about a new manager and his squad. For Rangers and Derek McInnes, the coming weeks will be an important period to address identifiable weaknesses, show tangible improvement and reset expectations as the season progresses.
