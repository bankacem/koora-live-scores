St Mirren surge: Two opening wins that challenge relegation predictions in the Scottish Premiership

8/8/2026 · By Koora Live

St Mirren have begun the new Scottish Premiership campaign in a way few neutral observers expected. BBC Sport reported that many had the Paisley club down as one of the likeliest sides to go down this season, yet they have opened with two victories — a start that already alters the early-season narrative.

This piece examines how those opening wins matter in context, why preseason projections can be misleading, and what the early momentum could mean for St Mirren as the campaign unfolds in Scotland's top flight.

Why expectations pointed to relegation

Preseason projections often blend historical form, squad depth, financial muscle and transfer activity into a simple verdict on who will thrive and who will struggle. For clubs like St Mirren, who operate with far fewer resources than the traditional big spenders in the Scottish Premiership, media and fan forecasts can quickly tip towards relegation as the default outcome. BBC Sport captured that mood by noting that St Mirren were, for many observers, among the favourites to drop down.

There are understandable reasons such assessments emerge. The Scottish Premiership is a small, competitive league where marginal gaps in quality or budget can have a pronounced effect across the course of a season. Newly promoted clubs, teams with damaged recent form or those who have lost key players are commonly targeted by pundits when predicting the relegation battle. That backdrop helps explain why a club with St Mirren’s profile might have been widely written off before a ball was kicked.

The significance of two opening wins

Winning the opening fixtures of a league campaign does more than deliver three points twice over. It provides a psychological lift for players, coach and supporters, briefly rewriting the broader storylines that surround a club. For a side labelled a relegation candidate, two consecutive victories can act as immediate counter-evidence — signalling resilience and the potential to overperform early on.

Tactically, early wins give a manager licence to build confidence and a clearer template for selection. In a league of 12 teams where form swings can be sudden, establishing a strong start reduces early-season pressure and can force rivals to rethink their approach when they face a side that looks organised and dangerous despite pre-season doubts. While two matches are a small sample, they are a valuable platform from which to stake a claim for safety and, at the very least, to push back against low expectations.

Analysis

It is tempting to over-read early results, but context matters. Two wins at the start of a campaign do not guarantee safety, nor do they completely overturn those preseason predictions. However, they do force a recalibration. For journalists, analysts and bookmakers who rely on preseason indicators, an immediate competitive response from a team labelled weak can expose the limits of projection-based narratives.

From a coaching perspective, the challenge is to translate positive momentum into sustained performance. Teams in the Scottish Premiership who have limited resources must be pragmatic — squeezing maximum value from tactical organisation, set-piece efficiency and team cohesion. If St Mirren can maintain the discipline and belief shown in those opening wins, they will convert early optimism into tangible progress. Conversely, if the wins were anomalous, the earlier relegation assessments could prove prescient again as the season progresses.

St Mirren’s opening two victories have already altered the conversation around their season. As BBC Sport noted, they began the campaign under the shadow of relegation predictions — the challenge now is to build on this early promise and let results over the full campaign determine whether those initial forecasts were wrong.
