Maguire urges Marcus Rashford to stay at Manchester United to boost Carrick's team

8/16/2026 · By Koora Live

Manchester United defender Harry Maguire has publicly expressed his hope that Marcus Rashford will remain at the club, saying Rashford can improve Michael Carrick's team. The comment was reported by BBC Sport and highlights internal calls for continuity around a player who has been a prominent figure in United's attacking ranks.

Maguire's message and the context reported by BBC Sport

According to BBC Sport, Harry Maguire said he hopes Marcus Rashford will stay with Manchester United because he believes the forward can improve Michael Carrick's team. Maguire, identified in the report as a Manchester United defender, framed his appeal around what Rashford brings to the squad rather than other external issues. The coverage by BBC Sport focuses on Maguire's position within the dressing room and his view that keeping key attacking options matters for the team's prospects.

The remark places a spotlight on internal dynamics at Old Trafford: senior players publicly backing teammates can be significant for squad morale and stability. While the BBC Sport piece supplies the core quote, it leaves room for interpretation over how that endorsement might influence decisions by players, staff or the club hierarchy.

What Rashford staying could mean for Carrick's team

Marcus Rashford is widely known as a forward for Manchester United who progressed through the club's youth system and has been used across the front line. Retaining a player of his profile typically preserves attacking options such as pace, directness and finishing ability, attributes often cited when clubs aim to sustain or increase their threat in the final third.

Michael Carrick, referenced in the BBC Sport excerpt as the leader of the team Maguire mentioned, has a background at the club as a former player who moved into coaching. If Rashford remains, analysts might see that as maintaining a proven attacking outlet that Carrick could deploy in a variety of tactical setups. Equally, keeping established squad members can help a coach manage rotation and adapt to the demands of domestic and European competitions, should the club be involved.
