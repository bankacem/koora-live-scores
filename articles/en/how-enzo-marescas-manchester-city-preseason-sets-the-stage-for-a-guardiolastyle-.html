How Enzo Maresca’s Manchester City Pre‑Season Sets the Stage for a Guardiola‑style Legacy

8/14/2026 · By Koora Live

With Pep Guardiola exiting after a historic run, Enzo Maresca steps into the spotlight as the next head coach of Manchester City. BBC Sport has taken a close look at the pre‑season fixtures and early tactical experiments to gauge how the new manager’s approach compares to the one he's inherited. The final scoreline of these warm‑up games isn’t the only metric – it’s the styles, player deployment, and squad readiness that will determine if Maresca can continue the club’s trajectory of on‑field dominance.

Maresca’s Tactical Blueprint: Initiation from the Front Bench

Maresca’s pre‑season outings were heavy on defensive organization and disciplined movement. The Italian manager emphasized structured pressing in the first third, a departure from some of the spontaneous bursts seen under Guardiola. In practice, City stretched wide, keeping midfield populous before advancing an overload with the antiphase to force out-of‑position defenders. This systematic phased approach to pressure suggests Maresca is favouring a more physically demanding framework that positives the midfield’s conditioning.

One standout element was the team’s flexible use of high‑line defenders. Through these games, City’s back three were asked to shift frontward as supporting players, turning the defensive trio into a lockdown shield. The pre‑season tape also highlighted Maresca’s reliance on the corner kicks that the team physically carries an average of 40% of the total possession. That version of possession-oriented play translates into fewer high‑intensity sprints, an intriguing sign the coach might intend to sustain over the full Premier League campaign.

These experiments illustrate the transformative mindset Maresca might bring: a balance of high‑press risk and structured compassion for player fitness, both of which are needed for a campaign—especially in a league that puts pressure on squad depth and mentality from the start.

The Guardiola Legacy: What City Reaches As a Benchmark

Pep Guardiola’s tenure at City redefined English football. His style is characterised by a low‑line, highly technical possession game that sees the ball used to dominate time and space. The Serb’s deep‑lying midfielders orchestrates the tempo while wide play lends a high‑pressing, propelling attack that typically pushes North‑South. Over the last seven seasons City has broken an array of domestic and European records – continually reflecting Guardiola’s sought after form of creative fluidity.

Guardiola’s early season match at Old Trafford, where City’s ball control was perceived as a surgical procedure, was a prime example of his refining of the footballing language. The full‑time-round strategy inclining to 4-2-3-1 was not only an expression of tactical expertise but also an embodiment of the club’s ambition: possession > 60% and pressing coverage > 30% field area.

Maresca is not new to the city; memories of the 2005-06 he had as a player and later as head coach cross the backboard. A known exponent of high‑press but often lower in intensity compared to Guardiola, Maresca’s tactical foundation may mirror a more flexible strategy with an eye on the game plans he studied during his time at Torino. That means City will be at a point where players will have to not only convert possession to goals but also maintain high levels of team concentration under the pre‑season phasing.

Analysis

Maresca’s recipe for success in the Premier League will rely on how quickly he can instill a cohesive style that maximises the squad’s innate strengths while capturing the modern game balance that Guardiola masters. By measuring early-footed fitness across match sessions and the attitude of the players towards high‑intensity dribbling, his pre‑season work appears to point out ways to restore the team’s confidence while preserving the mental robustness required in high‑profile fixtures. If his systems work on the warm‑up field, it suggests that City’s success may not revolve around a single technique but a composite of pressing discipline and dynamic possession.

The BBC’s early assessment signals that the new manager can keep the core of City’s tradition, however the full test will come in the early league matches when the tactical dynamism is truly required. Where Guardiola nurtured a brand of football with an almost constant foyer of decision corners, Maresca seems to position himself in an environment where battle‑testing durability of the roster is an essential factor.

With the new coach's pre‑season tactics signalling a commitment to disciplined pressing and fitness, the next chapters for Manchester City will be about how this evolution translates into real quick‑start league form. As the Premier League phase dawns, all eyes will question whether Maresca can build upon the Guardiola legacy or ground his own bolstered vision for City’s future.
