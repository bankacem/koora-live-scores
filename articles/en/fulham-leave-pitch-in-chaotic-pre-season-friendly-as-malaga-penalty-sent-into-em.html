Fulham leave pitch in chaotic pre-season friendly as Malaga penalty sent into empty net

8/13/2026 · By Koora Live

Fulham's pre-season friendly with Malaga ended in unusual and chaotic fashion after the Premier League club left the pitch before a penalty shootout could take place. According to BBC Sport, the sequence concluded with Malaga taking a penalty into an empty net while Fulham had already vacated the field.

The incident has attracted attention because such scenarios are rare in high-profile pre-season fixtures. While friendlies are typically used to build fitness and test tactics, the way this match finished has raised questions about communication, preparation and the protocols that surround exhibition games.

How the friendly concluded

The match in question, billed as a pre-season friendly, did not finish in the routine manner fans and coaches usually expect. As reported by BBC Sport, Fulham left the pitch prior to a scheduled penalty shootout and Malaga subsequently took a penalty with no opposing players present in the goal area. This sequence — teams preparing for a penalty decider following a friendly, followed by one side exiting the field — is not a common outcome for warm-up fixtures.

Pre-season friendlies can sometimes include non-standard elements such as rolling substitutions, short match durations, or even practice shootouts; these variations are typically agreed between the clubs and organisers beforehand. What made this occasion notable was the abrupt departure of one team before that concluding ritual, leading to the image of a penalty being taken into an empty net.

Context: what pre-season friendlies are for and why this mattered

Friendlies in the pre-season are primarily designed as preparation: they help managers assess squad fitness, try tactical ideas, integrate new signings and give match minutes to fringe players. Because these matches are not competitive fixtures in league or cup competitions, organisers often exercise flexibility over format and timing.

That flexibility can also create situations where misunderstandings or disputes carry more visible consequences. When a team leaves the pitch unexpectedly, even in a non-competitive match, it can disrupt the intended preparation for both sides and attract media scrutiny. In this case, BBC Sport's coverage highlighted the unusual ending — a penalty taken at an empty goal — which underlines how even friendly matches can become talking points when protocols break down or plans go awry.

Analysis

This episode serves as a reminder that pre-season fixtures, despite their unofficial status, still carry reputational and practical weight. Analysts might see the incident as symptomatic of communication gaps between clubs, match organisers and coaching teams. If one party expects a ritual such as a penalty shootout for practice or entertainment and the other does not, the result can be a public relations headache even before competitive football resumes.

It is also worth noting that because friendlies are governed by arrangements between the participating clubs or the hosting promoters, consequences or follow-up actions tend to be handled privately. Any interpretation of motive — whether a protest, logistical misunderstanding or other reason — should be framed as conjecture unless official statements are released by the clubs or organisers involved.

The end of the Fulham versus Malaga pre-season match — with Fulham leaving the pitch and Malaga taking a penalty into an empty net — provided an unexpected talking point in what would otherwise have been a routine warm-up encounter. As reported by BBC Sport, the incident underscores how quickly a preparatory fixture can attract attention when plans do not run to script, and it will likely prompt both clubs to address what happened behind the scenes.
