The remit of a FIFA president and why scrutiny follows

The president of FIFA serves as the public face and chief executive of world football’s governing body, responsible for setting strategic direction, representing FIFA to stakeholders and overseeing the organisation of flagship events such as the FIFA World Cup. As with other senior figures in sport, the role attracts attention from national associations, governments, commercial partners and the global media. That combination of visibility and influence means decisions by a president can rapidly become subjects of intense scrutiny.

The BBC Sport headline highlights themes often associated with top-level football administration — interaction with politicians, the use of private travel and the exercise of institutional power.
