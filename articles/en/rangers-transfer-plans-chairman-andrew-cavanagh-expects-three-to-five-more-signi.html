Rangers transfer plans: chairman Andrew Cavanagh expects three to five more signings

8/10/2026 · By Koora Live

Rangers chairman Andrew Cavanagh has expressed frustration with the club's start to the season and indicated that the club expects to make further additions before the current transfer window shuts. The remarks were reported by BBC Sport, which conveyed the chairman's view that three to five new signings are likely before the window closes.

What the chairman said and why it matters

According to a BBC Sport report, Rangers chairman Andrew Cavanagh has voiced frustration at how the season has begun and has suggested the club will be active in the transfer market, expecting three to five more signings prior to the close of the window. As the club's chair, Cavanagh speaks for the board and his comments underline a willingness to invest in the squad to address early-season concerns.

Public statements from a chairman can influence perceptions inside and outside the club. For supporters, such comments offer reassurance that the board recognises shortcomings and is prepared to act. For potential targets and agents, they signal that the club is seeking reinforcements and may be prepared to move quickly if a deal meets their requirements.

How additional signings could affect Rangers' season

Bringing in multiple players before a transfer window closes can alter a team's dynamics. New arrivals can provide fresh competition for places, cover for injury-prone areas, or introduce different tactical options for the manager. In a league with domestic and potential European commitments, depth is often viewed as essential to sustain a campaign over several months.

However, integrating three to five signings mid-season or during an already-running campaign carries challenges. New players need time to adapt to the manager's style, teammates and possibly a new country or league. The success of a recruitment spree depends on the quality of scouting, the fit between the profile of signings and team needs, and the speed with which the club can finalise and register deals before the window deadline.

Analysis

Cavanagh's public admission of frustration and the explicit expectation of multiple signings is a clear signal the board and sporting leaders want to act decisively. Analysts might view this as a pragmatic response to competitive pressures — boosting the squad can be a short-term remedy to early setbacks. That said, the ultimate impact will depend on recruitment quality and how quickly new players are assimilated. If the club secures targets who address specific weaknesses, the additions could stabilise and improve results; conversely, late-window activity that prioritises quantity over fit risks insufficient cohesion. Overall, the chairman's comments suggest urgency from the board and an intent to support the manager in reshaping the squad within the constraints of the transfer window.
