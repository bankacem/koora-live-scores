VAR’s Offside Oversight in Haaland’s Winning Goal Raises Questions

9/15/2026 · By Koora Live

A stunning turn of events unfolded at the weekend’s Premier League fixture when Erling Haaland’s dramatic winner sparked a debate that may reach the hallowed halls of football governance. Referees’ chief Howard Webb revealed that the video assistant referee did not even consider whether Enzo Fernandez was offside at the time of the goal. The announcement has reignited discussions about the integrity of VAR’s decision‑making process and how it safeguards the game’s fundamental rules.

The Controversial Goal

The early hours of Monday night turned into a nightmare for fans who believed that Haaland’s late strike had been earned at a spot where Enzo Fernandez had drifted out of his opponent’s play. In Premier League football, the offside rule is one of the most scrutinised and technically demanding criteria for a match‑deciding goal. The rule stipulates that a player in an advanced position is offside if any part of his head is level with or ahead of the last defender at the moment the ball is played. Refereeing laws require the assistant to throw a flag to signal an offside offence, after which the VAR team has to confirm the flag and then assess whether the positional criteria were met. The fact that the VAR process apparently halted without even debating the possibility of an offside position suggests that the re‑play and flagging protocol may have been bypassed at a critical moment.

Howard Webb’s Admission

Speaking to reporters on the back of the incident, Webb conceded that the VAR system did not thread the usual safety net of flagging before delivering the final outcome. The chief referee is known for his calm demeanor and long‑standing commitment to fair play. He has previously chaired discussions on improving the consistency of the VAR call, highlighting a need for a standard protocol that prevents such oversights. Webb’s statement underscores an alliance of fact and responsible governance: when a system designed to uphold the Laws of the Game has a lapse, the authority driving that system must own the misstep. Whether the ad‑hoc decision to bypass the flag was a human error, a technological glitch or a procedural oversight remains to be examined in detail by the Football Association’s refereeing committee.

Analysis

The mere fact that the video assistant referee failed to probe an offside position in a goal that was accepted is a serious procedural problem. Analysts might see this as a sign that the current system is still fragile and requires tighter safeguards. In the face of high‑profile matches where a shoulder‑to‑shoulder finish can win titles, the margin for error is minute. This incident could prompt the authorities to codify a mandatory flag‑before‑call rule, ensuring that every potential in‑play infraction is flagged and then checked before a decision is made. The next step could involve software upgrades that automatically detect bell signals from the assistant’s flag, thereby creating an audit trail that can’t be ignored. By instituting such a technologically assisted flag, referees might minimise the risk of an editorial bias or a momentary lapse from human operators.

Howard Webb’s admission leaves a window of opportunity for the Premier League and its governing bodies to re‑evaluate how the VAR system is employed at the highest level of football. By learning from this oversight, officials may work toward a future where every goal is scrutinised with the full rigor of the rulebook, preserving the spirit of fair competition for fans worldwide.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does the VAR system need an assistant’s flag?

The assistant referee’s flag serves as the first line of detection for offside, goal‑line, and hand‑ball incidents. Once the flag is thrown, the VAR team receives a clear cue that the rule has been flagged for review, ensuring that the camera replays focus on the correct event. This dual‑layer system is designed to reduce the risk of missing an infraction that could change the outcome of a match.

What role does the referee’s chief play in VAR oversight?

The referee’s chief, such as Howard Webb, is responsible for overseeing the technical and procedural aspects of match supervision. This includes ensuring that referee staff adhere to the prescribed sequence of calls and that VAR protocols are strictly followed. When a deviation occurs, the chief leads an analysis to determine whether an update to the guidelines is necessary.

How are offside decisions normally adjudicated in the Premier League?

While the offside rule is part of the Laws of the Game, the Premier League’s refereeing committee provides a specific interpretation that a player is offside if any part of their head is level with or ahead of the last opponent at the moment the ball is played. A flag must be raised by an assistant before the referee stops play and the video footage is reviewed. If the assistant fails to flag, the standard protocol does not permit a reorder of the situation solely based on the VAR team’s general observations.
