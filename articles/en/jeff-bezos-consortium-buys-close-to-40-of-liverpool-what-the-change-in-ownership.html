Jeff Bezos consortium buys close to 40% of Liverpool — what the change in ownership could mean for Liverpool FC

8/18/2026 · By Koora Live

BBC Sport reports that a consortium including Amazon founder Jeff Bezos has acquired close to 40% of Liverpool FC from Fenway Sports Group (FSG). The move marks a major piece of investment into one of England’s most storied clubs and represents a significant shift in the club's ownership structure as reported by the broadcaster.

This article unpacks what is publicly known from the BBC Sport report, places the sale in the wider context of Liverpool’s recent history, and explores the kinds of effects such an investment might have, based on general football business practice and precedent.

The confirmed facts: who sold and who bought

According to BBC Sport, Fenway Sports Group (FSG) sold almost 40% of Liverpool to a consortium that includes Jeff Bezos. That is the sole transaction detail publicly disclosed in the report: the buyer is a consortium with the Amazon founder among its members, and the seller is FSG. No further financial figures, exact percentages beyond “close to 40%,” or timing details are provided in the BBC Sport excerpt.

It is important to stick to those confirmed elements. Beyond that short statement, any additional specifics about terms, valuation, or governance arrangements would need to come from official club releases or further reporting. What can be said with confidence is that the sale involves a sizeable minority stake — large enough to attract attention and discussion about strategic direction and investment priorities.

Context: Liverpool FC, Fenway Sports Group and modern football investment

Liverpool FC is one of England’s most recognisable football clubs, founded in the late 19th century and based at Anfield. The club has a long history of domestic and European success and a global fanbase. Fenway Sports Group became the club’s owners in 2010, a widely reported change of ownership that has overseen some of the club’s most successful recent years.

In modern football, sales of minority stakes to high-profile investors or consortiums are increasingly common. Such deals can be structured to provide fresh capital, expand commercial reach, or bring strategic partners with global networks. While the BBC Sport piece confirms the involvement of Jeff Bezos in a buying group, how that will translate into operational influence, board representation, or commercial initiatives at Liverpool will depend on the specific terms of the transaction — details not included in the report.
