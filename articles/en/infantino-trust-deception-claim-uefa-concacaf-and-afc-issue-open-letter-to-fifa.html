Infantino trust 'deception' claim: UEFA, CONCACAF and AFC issue open letter to FIFA

8/10/2026 · By Koora Live

BBC Sport reports that UEFA, Concacaf and the Asian Football Confederation have jointly accused FIFA of breaking trust "through deception" in an open letter. The allegation, attributed in coverage to confederation presidents, names Gianni Infantino in its headline and has prompted renewed attention to relations between world football's governing body and its regional members.

The open letter and who made the accusation

According to BBC Sport, the charge that FIFA broke trust "through deception" was made in an open letter issued by the presidents of UEFA, Concacaf and the Asian Football Confederation (AFC). An open letter is a public statement addressed to an individual or organisation, intended to communicate a message to a wide audience rather than to be a private correspondence. In this case, those leading three of FIFA's six continental confederations chose that route to make a formal allegation public. The BBC Sport headline explicitly linked the allegation to Gianni Infantino, who is widely known as FIFA's president.

Why regional confederations matter in world football

UEFA, Concacaf and the AFC are three of the continental confederations that make up the global structure of football governance, each representing hundreds of national associations and organising regional competitions. UEFA covers Europe, Concacaf covers North America, Central America and the Caribbean, and the AFC covers the Asian continent. These bodies play a crucial role in administering competitions, developing the game at national levels and representing member associations' interests at the global level.

When continental presidents speak collectively, it signals an important dynamic within international football governance because confederations are key stakeholders in FIFA's policy-making and strategic direction. Public disputes between FIFA and its confederations can affect how stakeholders perceive the organisation's leadership, transparency and decision-making processes.
