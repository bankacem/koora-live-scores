Memphis Depay takes pay cut to stay at Corinthians after contract row

8/18/2026 · By Koora Live

Memphis Depay, the former Manchester United striker, has agreed to take a pay cut to remain at Brazilian club Corinthians, BBC Sport reports. The move comes after he waived a reported fee of nearly £3m, an action that followed his accusation that the club engaged in "unacceptable behaviour" during talks over a contract extension.

The facts as reported

According to BBC Sport, Depay relinquished a near £3m payment and accepted reduced terms in order to continue his spell with Corinthians. The decision came just days after he publicly criticised the club, using the phrase "unacceptable behaviour" to describe how discussions over a new contract had been handled. The coverage makes clear that the two developments — the accusation and the subsequent waiver of the fee — are connected in timing, though the outlet does not set out further internal details of the negotiations.

Context: Depay and Corinthians in perspective

Memphis Depay is widely known for his career in European football before moving to Brazil. He has previously played for clubs including PSV Eindhoven and Manchester United, and later had spells at Lyon and Barcelona; he is also a senior Dutch international. Corinthians, officially Sport Club Corinthians Paulista, are one of Brazil's most prominent clubs, founded in 1910 and based in São Paulo, with a large and passionate fanbase.

Contract negotiations between high-profile players and clubs can be complex, involving wages, signing-on sums, performance clauses and image rights. Public disputes or critical statements are not unprecedented in world football, and how they are resolved — whether by a player taking a financial hit, the club altering its stance, or both sides finding a compromise — can set the tone for the relationship going forward.

Analysis

This sequence — a public criticism followed by a financial concession — can be read in a number of ways. On one hand, the player’s readiness to forgo a substantial sum may indicate a desire to prioritise stability and playing time, or a wish to avoid a prolonged dispute that could distract both parties. On the other hand, the fact that Depay felt compelled to speak out about the negotiation process suggests there were tensions behind the scenes that required public airing to reach a resolution. Analysts might view the episode as a reminder that even high-profile transfers and extensions are vulnerable to breakdowns in communication and trust.

For Corinthians, securing the player's commitment despite the row might be seen as a pragmatic outcome: the club retains a recognized attacking figure while simultaneously defusing an immediate public relations problem. For Depay, the decision to accept a reduced deal could reflect personal priorities common to established professionals — such as competitive fit, lifestyle preference or broader career planning — rather than purely financial calculation. These are interpretive angles rather than confirmed motives, and they highlight the multiple factors that influence contract decisions in modern football.

The BBC Sport account shows a high-profile player and club moving quickly from public disagreement to an agreement that involves a financial compromise. While the report outlines the key actions — the accusation of "unacceptable behaviour" and the waiver of nearly £3m — it leaves wider motives open to interpretation, underlining how contract rows in football can produce sudden and pragmatic settlements.
