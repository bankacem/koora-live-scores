Man City Maintains Claim: Titles ‘Absolutely Not’ Tainted After Premier League Verdict

10/9/2026 · By Koora Live

Following a Premier League disciplinary inquiry, Manchester City were found guilty of the majority of 115 charges filed against them. In a confident statement, manager Enzo Maresca insisted that the club’s trophies are "absolutely not" tainted. The controversy surrounding these allegations has stirred debate among fans and pundits alike, prompting the club’s leadership to urgently defend their record.

Maresca’s Defiant Response to the Inquiry

Enzo Maresca, who took over the managerial reins after Pep Guardiola’s departure, appeared on television to address the Premier League’s findings. He stressed that the negative outcome in only a fraction of the accusations should not colour the club’s achievements, describing the titles as “absolutely not” compromised. Maresca’s remarks were aimed at reassuring the club’s supporters that their triumphs remain legitimate. He highlighted the rigorous investigative process and implied that any wrongdoing was minimal or irrelevant to the resultant honours. The club’s official statement, in line with the manager’s stance, reaffirmed their commitment to transparency and fairness.

What the Verdict Means for Manchester City’s Legacy

The investigation, part of the Premier League’s ongoing efforts to uphold the sport’s integrity, encompassed 115 distinct charges ranging from alleged issues of fairness to procedural irregularities. Although the majority of these were upheld, the club was cleared of many offences, which Maresca now argues means the trophies remain unblemished. Historically, Manchester City—founded in 1880 and based at the Etihad Stadium—has experienced a meteoric rise since the early 2010s, securing multiple Premier League titles under former manager Pep Guardiola. Post‑Guardiola, the club’s sustainability and reputation remain at the top of the discussion as they seek to build on a record of success while navigating the complexities of modern football governance.

Analysis

From a strategic viewpoint, Maresca’s insistence that the club’s titles are untainted aligns with the federation’s broader objective of preserving competitive balance and public confidence. By framing the verdict as a remnant of a larger, largely exonerated context, the club positions itself as a victim of disproportionate scrutiny rather than a guilty party. Analysts might see this narrative as a calculated move to maintain brand integrity, especially as the Premier League sports a global audience that demands both spectacle and fairness.

The outcome also reflects a broader trend in professional football where clubs facing regulatory action must quickly pivot to crisis communication. By insisting that their honours remain intact, Manchester City reinforces the message that past successes are grounded in on‑field performance rather than off‑field controversies—a narrative likely to resonate with long‑time supporters and new fans alike.

With the rulings in hand, Manchester City and their new manager are continuing to defend their achievements as legitimate, asserting that the trophies earned under recent decades stand proud. As the football world watches, the club’s next steps will likely focus on reinforcing both on‑pitch success and the perception of a fair sporting landscape.
