FPL Strategy: How to Use BBC Sport’s Eight Expert Picks to Build a Winning Fantasy Premier League Squad

8/17/2026 · By Koora Live

BBC Sport’s Fantasy Premier League experts have identified eight players they believe can give managers a strong foundation for a successful FPL season. While the publication supplies a focused shortlist, managers still need to fit any recommended names into their own budgets, formation plans and strategic timeline.

Why an expert shortlist matters for your FPL planning

Shortlists produced by experienced analysts are useful because they distil a lot of scouting into a manageable number of options. BBC Sport’s experts have weighed up playing time, role security and attacking or defensive involvement to present eight players they think will help managers. Using a curated list can save time during the busy transfer windows and give managers a set of reliable starting points.

However, expert selections should be seen as guidance rather than a one-size-fits-all prescription. Every FPL manager has different budget constraints, chip plans (Bench Boost, Triple Captain, Free Hit) and risk tolerances. The value of an expert pick depends on how it fits into your overall squad construction and fixture analysis.

Practical steps to integrate the eight BBC Sport picks into your squad

Start by checking how each recommended player slots into your team structure: which position they occupy, whether they are a nailed starter for their club, and whether they’re involved in set-pieces or penalties. Balance is key — don’t overload on one club or position early in the season. Use one or two of the expert selections as core players and build around them with budget enablers and differential picks.

Monitor rotation risk and upcoming fixtures before making transfers. Experts’ picks are often chosen because they marry minutes with goal or clean-sheet potential, but schedules change, and managers must react to blank or double gameweeks. Finally, use the transfer window and chips strategically: bringing in a highly recommended player ahead of a favourable run of fixtures or saving a transfer for a double gameweek can make a significant difference.

Analysis

BBC Sport’s list of eight players offers a focused starting point, and managers can gain an advantage by understanding why those names were chosen: mainly a combination of minutes, role clarity and involvement in key phases of play. That said, relying solely on any single expert list removes the advantage of tailoring moves to your own team and schedule. A sensible approach is to adopt some expert recommendations while cross-checking them against fixture difficulty, your chip timeline and team balance.

Another practical angle is timing. Early-season transfers are often influenced by preseason reports and early form, whereas mid-season moves hinge on injuries, tactical changes and wildcards. Treat expert picks as a high-probability shortlist to inform timing and prioritisation, not as an automatic template to copy verbatim.

BBC Sport’s experts have narrowed the field to eight players they believe will aid Fantasy Premier League managers. Use that shortlist as a helpful input, but adapt each recommendation to your own budget, fixture analysis and long-term strategy to maximise your chances across the season.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who created the list of eight players?

The eight-player shortlist was produced by BBC Sport’s Fantasy Premier League experts, who selected players they think can help managers have a successful FPL season.

Can I add all eight recommendations to my squad at once?

Whether you can include all eight depends on your budget, formation and existing squad composition. It is common practice to adopt a few expert selections as core players and complement them with budget enablers, while keeping flexibility for future transfers and chip use.

How should I use expert picks alongside my own research?

Use expert picks as a starting point: verify each candidate’s minutes, role (set-pieces, penalties), upcoming fixtures and rotation risk. Match these factors with your chip strategy and budget to decide when to bring players in. Experts provide informed suggestions, but the best results usually come from combining that guidance with personalised planning.
