De Zerbi and Tottenham: how the transfer window could lift Spurs up the Premier League table

8/5/2026 · By Koora Live

Roberto De Zerbi, the Tottenham Hotspur manager, has told BBC Sport he believes the transfer window can be a vehicle for improvement as he looks to steer his side back up the Premier League table. The BBC's headline even dubbed him the 'Shark', capturing the idea of an intense, proactive approach to recruitment.

De Zerbi has described a clear affection for the transfer market, and that enthusiasm shapes how he views squad building and short-term course correction. BBC Sport reported his self-confessed love of the transfer window and his hope that activity there can help Tottenham improve their league standing.

De Zerbi's transfer-window mindset

The BBC Sport report highlights Roberto De Zerbi's open admission that he enjoys working in the transfer window and sees it as a strategic opportunity. For any manager operating in the modern game, the transfer market is both a chance to plug weaknesses and to alter the tactical profile of a team. De Zerbi's approach, as presented, appears to treat recruitment not simply as squad maintenance but as an extension of his coaching remit.

That perspective is common among contemporary managers who favour targeted additions to complement their style. The manager's stated enthusiasm suggests he will be actively involved in identifying players who can fit specific roles on the pitch, adapt to his methods and potentially change the trajectory of a season. While the BBC piece focuses on his attitudes rather than confirmed business, it underlines a readiness to use the window as a lever for progress.

What a transfer strategy could mean for Tottenham's campaign

Tottenham Hotspur are a Premier League club with a large fan base and considerable expectations. The top-flight campaign is long and demanding, and smart recruitment can make a measurable difference in squad depth, injury cover and tactical options. If De Zerbi and the club prioritise coherent signings — players who can slot into his system and bolster areas of weakness — the cumulative effect might be improved results.

However, transfers are inherently uncertain: incoming players need time to settle, adapt to new teammates and adjust to the intensity of the Premier League. Any change in league position attributed to new signings would therefore be a matter of progression over weeks and months rather than instant transformation. BBC Sport's coverage emphasises De Zerbi's hope and intent, which supporters and observers will read as an ambition to use the window proactively.

Analysis

Framed as analysis rather than fact: De Zerbi's self-confessed enthusiasm for the transfer market suggests he views recruitment as central to implementing his footballing philosophy at Tottenham. Analysts might see this as an indication that the manager will push for additions tailored to his tactical demands rather than generic squad upgrades.
