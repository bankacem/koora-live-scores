Savinho's £75m Move to Tottenham: Insights into Manchester City's Multi-Club Model

8/23/2026 · By Koora Live

The £75m move of Brazilian starlet Savinho from Manchester City to Tottenham has turned heads across the football world. According to BBC Sport, the transfer raises questions about the success of City's multi-club model, spearheaded by the Abu Dhabi ownership. This high-profile deal highlights the intricate mechanisms behind modern football operations and how City Football Group is redefining player development.

Savinho's Transfer: A £75m Statement

At just 19 years old, Savinho has already cemented himself as one of football's most coveted young talents. His £75m transfer to Tottenham underscores both his potential and the increasingly globalized nature of the modern transfer market. Having been a part of Manchester City's vast football network, Savinho's career represents the benefits of being developed within a structure designed to nurture talent on an international scale. The sizable transfer sum also reflects Tottenham's belief in his capabilities and growth potential as they seek to bolster their squad for the future.

Manchester City's Multi-Club Model Explained

Manchester City's owners, the City Football Group (CFG), have become pioneers in the footballing world with their unique approach to talent development and club ownership. With investments in clubs across multiple continents, such as Girona FC in Spain, New York City FC in the U.S., and Melbourne City FC in Australia, CFG leverages this global network to identify, develop, and transfer players across different leagues and levels of competition. The transfer of Savinho is a prime example of how the multi-club model can be used to drive both sporting and financial success. The system allows players to gain valuable experience in competitive environments and mature before making a significant move, in this case to Tottenham.

Analysis

The £75m sale of Savinho suggests that Manchester City's multi-club model continues to bear fruit. By cultivating talent within their global network, City are not only developing players for their own first team but also creating sellable assets. In the case of Savinho, his departure to Tottenham indicates that CFG's investments in scouting and development are yielding significant returns. While Spurs undoubtedly aim to gain a long-term star, City may see this as the validation of their approach to producing elite-level players who are market-ready for major European clubs. However, this transfer also invites questions about whether the City Football Group prioritizes financial profit over integrating young talents into their first team—a debate likely to persist with each marquee sale.

Savinho's record-breaking move to Tottenham is a testament to the visionary approach of Manchester City's multi-club model. While it serves as proof of CFG's ability to generate top talent and financial returns, it also adds fuel to the ongoing debate about the balance between developing players for profit versus on-field contributions. As football increasingly adopts innovative models, the transfer of emerging stars like Savinho will remain pivotal in shaping the sport's future.
