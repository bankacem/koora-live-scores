BBC Sport has published a new instalment of its identity quiz titled 'Who am I? Guess Premier League star No 31.' The simple brief given to readers is to work out the identity of today's player in as few attempts as possible. This piece explains what that prompt means, offers general background on these kinds of Premier League quizzes and gives practical strategies to help readers approach the challenge.

What the BBC Sport 'Who am I?' feature is

The BBC Sport feature places a spotlight on a single Premier League player and invites readers to identify them based on a sequence of clues or images. In this edition, numbered No 31, the task set out by the outlet is concise: work out the identity of today's player in as few attempts as possible. This type of interactive content is designed to test fan knowledge, spark debate and encourage repeat visits as fans attempt to guess under limited attempts.

Why fans enjoy identity quizzes and how they typically work

Identity quizzes for footballers are popular because they combine trivia, observational skills and the thrill of recognition. Common formats include a series of progressively easier hints, cropped photos, career milestones or lists of clubs associated with a player. The objective — in this case to name the Premier League star in as few attempts as possible — introduces a competitive element, whether against a personal best or the wider fanbase.

Such quizzes also serve as light, accessible content for a broad audience: casual supporters might rely on iconic visual clues, while more engaged followers draw on detailed knowledge like positional play, transfer history or distinctive traits. Media outlets like BBC Sport use these quizzes to engage readers in a quick, sharable format that complements match reports and in-depth features.

Analysis

Quizzes like BBC Sport's 'Who am I? Guess Premier League star No 31' tap into football supporters' appetite for quick, interactive content. They reward both visual memory and broader knowledge of the domestic league — from club colours and stadiums to player appearances and roles. For publishers, these features increase engagement and time on site; for readers, they provide a short, satisfying test of expertise that can be repeated frequently. Analysts might see this format as part of a wider trend toward bite-sized, participatory sports journalism that complements longer analytical pieces.

BBC Sport's 'Who am I? Guess Premier League star No 31' presents a concise, engaging challenge: identify today's player in as few attempts as possible. Use observational skills, process of elimination and a measured guessing strategy to increase your chances — and most importantly, enjoy the test of your Premier League knowledge.
