Real Madrid agree £120m Diomande deal — transfer roundup as Liverpool and Aston Villa make moves

8/5/2026 · By Koora Live

Real Madrid have reportedly reached a £120m agreement for Yan Diomande, while Liverpool are exploring a move for Ibrahim Mbaye and Aston Villa have reached an agreement to sign Matteo Ruggeri, BBC Sport reports. These developments form part of a fast-moving transfer landscape that could reshape squads across Europe if completed.

Real Madrid's headline capture: what the £120m agreement means

According to BBC Sport, Real Madrid have reached a £120m agreement for Yan Diomande. Such a headline figure underlines the club's continued readiness to invest heavily when targeting players they view as long-term additions. Real Madrid are one of the most successful clubs in European football history, frequently active at the top end of the transfer market as they look to maintain competitive advantage both domestically and in continental competitions.

While the BBC report supplies the core fact of the agreement, the next practical steps in any high-profile transfer typically involve finalising personal terms, completing medical checks and registering the player with the relevant league and association. Until those elements are confirmed publicly, clubs and supporters usually treat transfer stories as subject to standard formalities and potential late changes.

Liverpool interest and Aston Villa agreement: Premier League activity

BBC Sport also reports that Liverpool are exploring a move for Ibrahim Mbaye. The phrasing 'explore' suggests initial interest or preliminary enquiries rather than a finished transaction. Liverpool, as a major Premier League club, have a global scouting network and routinely investigate a range of targets to strengthen their squad across multiple positions.

Separately, Aston Villa have reached an agreement to sign Matteo Ruggeri, according to BBC Sport. Villa have been active in recent windows as they look to build a competitive roster for domestic and cup commitments. Reaching an agreement generally signals that two clubs have found common ground on a transfer, though it is still subject to the usual processes — including agreement on personal terms with the player and any regulatory clearance required by the governing bodies.

Analysis

These three items reported by BBC Sport reflect two common patterns in modern football transfer windows: headline marquee deals and exploratory interest. Meanwhile, top clubs like Liverpool routinely investigate numerous options, signalling intent without committing until due diligence is complete. For clubs like Aston Villa, reported agreements indicate active recruitment to close perceived gaps in the squad.
