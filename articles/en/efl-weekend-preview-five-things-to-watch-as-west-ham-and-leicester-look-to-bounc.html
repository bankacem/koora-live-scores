Fallen giants versus ambitious upstarts: what the framing means

The phrase "fallen giants and ambitious upstarts" captures a familiar story in English football: established clubs that have enjoyed prolonged success or top-tier status can hit turbulent patches, while smaller or younger projects steadily climb and threaten the established order. BBC Sport uses that framing to highlight five items to monitor in the EFL this weekend, and it is a useful lens for supporters trying to read momentum and morale across the fixture list.

When a headline singles out clubs such as West Ham and Leicester as teams that "look to bounce back," it taps into that narrative of recovery. Both clubs are well-known names in the English game: West Ham United is a London-based club founded in 1895 that plays at the London Stadium, and Leicester City — founded in 1884 — has its base in the East Midlands at the King Power Stadium. That historical pedigree is part of what makes any dip in form feel like a fall from an established pedestal, and why their response over a weekend of fixtures can attract extra attention.

What does the term 'EFL' refer to?

EFL stands for the English Football League, the governing body and competition structure that runs the three professional divisions below the top tier of English football. It is commonly used to refer to the Championship, League One and League Two.

What does it mean when a club 'looks to bounce back'?

To "look to bounce back" generally means that a club is aiming to recover from a period of poor results or a dip in form. It implies the team is seeking an immediate positive response — often via tactical adjustments, improved performances or stronger results — to restore confidence and momentum.
