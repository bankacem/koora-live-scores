EFL warns losses are 'not sustainable' after PFA legal action — financial strain on English football pyramid

8/14/2026 · By Koora Live

The English Football League has warned that the current level of financial losses across the professional game is unsustainable, a concern made public in the wake of legal action brought by the Professional Footballers' Association. According to BBC Sport, EFL chief executive Trevor Birch said the scale of losses creates "a systemic insolvency risk" for the football pyramid.

This development places financial governance and the health of clubs under renewed scrutiny. While details of the PFA legal action are contained in reports, the central warning from the EFL leadership highlights risks that could potentially affect a wide range of clubs across the divisions.

What the EFL statement means and who is speaking

The comments were reported by BBC Sport and attributed to Trevor Birch, the EFL's chief executive. His phrase that losses amount to "a systemic insolvency risk" signals a concern not just about isolated clubs but about the interconnected structure of English football, commonly referred to as the football pyramid.

The EFL oversees the three divisions below the Premier League, where clubs vary hugely in size, revenue streams and financial resilience. When league leadership raises the prospect of systemic insolvency, it generally refers to a situation where multiple clubs face the threat of failing to meet their financial obligations, which can have knock-on effects across competitions, scheduling and employment in the game.

Context: PFA legal action and the broader financial landscape

BBC Sport's headline links the EFL warning to legal action initiated by the Professional Footballers' Association (PFA). The PFA is the players' union representing professional footballers in England and Wales; legal disputes involving unions and governing bodies can cover a range of issues from contractual matters to welfare and employment rights.

Regardless of the specific legal points, the intersection of legal challenges and public warnings about losses draws attention to the broader financial pressures clubs face — income disparities between leagues, matchday and commercial volatility, and the fixed costs of running professional teams. These pressures can prompt debate about regulation, financial controls and support mechanisms to preserve clubs and the competitions they contest.

Analysis

This warning from the EFL, as reported by BBC Sport, underscores a recurring theme in English football: the tension between sporting ambition and financial sustainability. Analysts might view the combination of a union-led legal challenge and a public statement from the league as a catalyst for renewed discussion about regulation — from spending controls to insolvency procedures and support frameworks for clubs at risk.

If multiple clubs were to face insolvency simultaneously, the operational and reputational consequences could be significant for competitions and communities. Policymakers, league administrators and stakeholder groups will likely face pressure to explore measures that reduce systemic risk while balancing competitive integrity and the livelihoods tied to the game.

The EFL's warning, reported by BBC Sport and voiced by Trevor Birch, brings financial sustainability back to the forefront of the football conversation. With the PFA's legal action also in play, stakeholders will be watching closely as clubs, unions and governing bodies respond to concerns about the health of the pyramid.
