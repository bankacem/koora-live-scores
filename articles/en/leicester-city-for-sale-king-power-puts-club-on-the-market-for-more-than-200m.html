Leicester City for sale: King Power puts club on the market for more than £200m

8/13/2026 · By Koora Live

BBC Sport reports that King Power, the Thai owners of Leicester City, are looking to sell the club after 16 years in charge, and are seeking in excess of £200m. The announcement marks a significant moment for a club that has risen from lower-league status to high-profile success in English football under King Power's stewardship.

A brief recap of King Power’s era at Leicester City

King Power took control of Leicester City 16 years ago and have been associated with the club ever since. During that period the club has experienced some of the most memorable moments in its modern history, building infrastructure and a global profile that stretches beyond its local roots. King Power itself is a Thai duty-free retailer, and the company name is also attached to Leicester’s home ground, a common indicator of the commercial and civic ties between owner and club. These are widely known, established aspects of the club’s recent history and provide context for why the potential sale is notable.

What the sale announcement means in practical terms

The reported asking price — more than £200m, according to BBC Sport — sets a clear starting point for any prospective purchasers. A sale of an English professional football club typically involves multiple steps beyond agreeing a headline price: due diligence on finances and operations, approval from the relevant football authorities, and negotiations over stadium and community commitments. While the precise timetable and interested parties have not been disclosed by the source, the public confirmation that King Power are 'hoping to sell' signals the beginning of an ownership transition process that will attract attention from both domestic and international investors.

Analysis

The decision by King Power to offer Leicester City for sale opens several strategic considerations. From a sporting perspective, new ownership can bring fresh investment, altered transfer philosophies and changes to long-term planning; at the same time, transitions carry risks around continuity, infrastructure projects and community relationships. Commercially, the valuation implied by the reported asking price reflects not only the team's on-field potential but also stadium arrangements, brand value and global reach. Observers should expect a period of speculation as agents and potential buyers assess whether the club fits their portfolio and ambitions. It will also be important to monitor how any prospective buyer addresses regulatory approvals and the existing commitments to supporters and local stakeholders.

King Power’s move to put Leicester City on the market for more than £200m, as reported by BBC Sport, begins a new chapter for the club. Over the coming months, supporters and observers will be watching closely to see who steps forward and how a change of ownership might shape Leicester City’s future on and off the pitch.
